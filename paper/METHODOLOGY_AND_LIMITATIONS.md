# CAFR paper — methodology and limitations

**Status:** methods/limitations source for the **executed** CAFR work. It documents
Step 5 (attribution gates) and Step 6 (C2-only reliability-gated repair), not the
original full-CAFR design. **No new experiment, dataset, test run, source change, or
configuration change was made for this document.** The Step 5 overall **FAIL** and
**E-8** stand unchanged. The original claim that full CAFR beats accuracy-based
selection at equal churn is **not** supported by these experiments and is retired.
Built 2026-10-18 from the frozen repository artifacts; Step 5 values corrected
2026-10-21 from the 2026-10-20 rerun.

**Scope in one sentence:** a synthetic-panel-only, seed-42 study of which injected
forecast-error causes the live monitor can attribute, followed by a C2-only
feasibility test of a one-time reliability-gated scale reset.

---

## 1. Study scope and research questions

### 1.1 What was actually run

Two experiments were run:

1. **Step 5 — attribution gates.** The full labelled synthetic panel was passed
   through the real pipeline: arm (c)'s forecaster, the rolling inventory simulator,
   the outcome monitor, and the priority-ordered attribution rule. The question was
   whether the monitor could name each injected cause at predeclared recall,
   false-positive, confusion, and priority-sensitivity bars.
2. **Step 6 — C2-only repair feasibility.** Conditional on the live monitor making
   a first post-injection C2 attribution, a logistic reliability gate decides whether
   to apply one C2-specific remedy. The paired outcome is inventory cost and cycle
   service level against the same policy with the C2 miscalibration left in place.

### 1.2 Research questions

- **RQ1 (Step 5):** On the labelled synthetic panel, which injected causes does the
  live monitor/attribution pipeline detect and name at the predeclared bars?
- **RQ2 (Step 6):** When the live monitor attributes C2, can a gate trained on Step 5
  readings decide whether to reset C2's interval scale, and what happens to cost and
  cycle service level relative to the untouched baseline?

### 1.3 What is outside the scope

The following parts of the original CAFR design were **not** executed as a whole and
are not tested here:

- full multi-cause CAFR versus accuracy-based selection at equal churn;
- the bandit, churn control, or any arm-to-arm comparison;
- multi-cause remedy selection or a six-cause attribution claim;
- real-data (RUF/M5) transfer, cost optimisation, or a multi-seed replication.

Both Step 5 and Step 6 use **arm (c)**, the accuracy-based adaptive-selection policy,
as the base policy. Step 6 adds a C2-specific scale provider to that same policy.
Neither experiment compares full CAFR against arm (c).

## 2. Step 5 experimental setup

### 2.1 Data generation and sample

The synthetic panel is generated by `cafr/data/synth/labels.py` from
`cafr/data/synth/generator.py` and `cafr/data/synth/injections.py`. The baseline DGP
is intermittent demand:

```
delta_t ~ Bernoulli(p)
z_t     ~ Gamma(shape=k, scale=mu_z/k)
y_t     = delta_t * z_t
```

Each SKU is assigned one of four reachable ADI × CV² cells. `p` is drawn from
0.2501–0.50 for the moderate-ADI cells and 0.05–0.25 for the high-ADI cells; `k` is
drawn from the corresponding low- or high-dispersion range; `mu_z` is uniform on
[5, 200]. Per-SKU statics are lead time ∈ {1, 2, 4}, unit cost from a
lognormal(2, 0.5), and holding rate uniform on [0.05, 0.30].

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Periods per SKU | 200 |
| Injection time `tau_inj` | 120 |
| Burn-in | 24 |
| Seed | 42 (held-out seed 43 was not used) |
| Panel generated | 2,000 SKUs: C0 200, C1–C7 200 each, C2b 200, mixture 200 |
| Panel used in Step 5 | 1,800 SKUs: C0 200, C1–C7 200 each, C2b 200 |
| Excluded from Step 5 | 200 C3 × C2 mixture (`ambiguous`) SKUs |
| C2 pooled denominator | 400 (C2 200 + C2b 200) |

Each series is generated from
`SeedSequence(seed, spawn_key=(panel_id, index))`, so its draws do not depend on how
many other series were generated. Panel IDs are control 0, single-cause 1, mixture 2,
and C2b 3.

The panel is a **demand panel**: `demand_observed = demand_true`, and
`stockout_flag` is False at panel level. Stock-outs are created only in the
simulator's counterfactual cost layer. The policy uses `observation_model: demand`,
so it sees the demand series; `unmet_model` is `lost_sales`.

The pooled forecaster is fitted once on the **first 24 periods of all 1,800 Step 5
SKUs**, including the SKUs later used as test SKUs in Step 6. Step 5 has no
train/validation/test split; all 1,800 SKUs are measured. This exposure is a
limitation (§6.6).

### 2.2 Causes and injections

| Cause | What the injection changes | Key recorded parameters |
|---|---|---|
| C0 | Nothing; control | No injection |
| C1 | Size mean `mu_z`: linear ramp over 40 periods after `tau` | `phi` ∈ {−0.4, −0.2, +0.2, +0.4}; `p` and `k` untouched |
| C2 | Forecaster's reported interval, not demand: `Q~ = mu_hat + gamma (Q_alpha − mu_hat)` | `gamma` solved per SKU; target coverage gap 0.12 at `alpha=0.95`, `R=3`; bounds (0.02, 8.0) |
| C2b | Intended modelling-error variant of C2 | Per-period sigma half-width applied to the aggregate interval through arm (c); demand and point forecast untouched. **Corrected; see §3.3** |
| C3 | Occurrence probability `p`: step at `tau` | `p_new = clip(p ± 0.15, 0.03, 0.60)`; sizes untouched |
| C4 | Size dispersion: `k → k × factor`, fixed `mu_z` | `factor` ∈ {0.25, 4.0}; `k` resampled to stay in the same CV² band |
| C5 | Abrupt step in `mu_z` or `p` at `tau` | Step scaled to the detectability budget; occurrence branch used only when feasible |
| C6 | Slow drift in `p` over 60 periods after `tau` | Ends near 0.35 if `p < 0.25`, else near 0.15; `mu_z` untouched; ADI crosses 4 |
| C7 | Policy's safety factor only | `safety_scale = 0.60`; demand untouched |

For C2, `_solve_gamma` bisects on Monte-Carlo coverage (20,000 aggregate draws) at
`alpha=0.95`, `R=3`, target `tau_cov + margin = 0.10 + 0.02 = 0.12`. The target is
not always reachable: **193/200** C2 SKUs clear 0.12, 4 land in [0.10, 0.12), and 3
fall below 0.10; all seven shortfalls hit the 0.02 gamma floor. All 200 C2 SKUs have
`gamma < 1` (range 0.020–0.560; median 0.453). The generator's 95% coverage-gap
median is −0.120.

The **monitor** checks a 90% conformal interval
(`detection.c2_interval_alpha = 0.90`), scaled by the same `gamma`. The generator's
95% solve and the monitor's 90% check are different coverage objects and are not
equated.

### 2.3 Pipeline and key frozen settings

Each SKU is run through arm (c):

- the pooled forecaster supplies `mu_hat`;
- arm (c) selects the active pool member by trailing MASE;
- the simulator computes `S = R * mu_hat + Q_alpha(R-period sums of signed
  residuals)`, with `R = L + 1 = 3`;
- the monitor is updated every period;
- attribution is priority-ordered and returns a cause plus fired rules, evidence,
  margins, and confidence.

Key frozen settings (from `cafr/configs/base.yaml` and `synthetic.yaml`):

| Area | Settings |
|---|---|
| Inventory | `L=2`, `R=3`, review period 1, protection convention `L+1`, lost sales, demand observation, `alpha=0.95`, `B/H=19`, quantile window 24, minimum 4 aggregates |
| Monitor checks | Start at burn-in 24; every `stride=4` periods (44 checks over periods 24–196) |
| Monitor windows | `W_short=24`, `W_sign=96`, `W_cal=30`, `W_occ=36`, `W_sz=24`, `W_class=30`, `W_svc=30`, `W_cusum=96`, `W_base=24` |
| Evidence floors | C1 8 nonzero; C3 10; C4 10; C2 30 coverage observations; C6 30 periods; C7 30 periods / 2 unmet cycles; C5 8 baseline nonzero |
| Detection thresholds | `|z_b| > 1.96`; `binomial_p=0.05`; `tau_cov=0.10`; `tau_occ=0.10`; C2 interval level 0.90; `delta_svc=0.10`; `ADI*=4.0`; `CV2*=0.49`; CUSUM `k=0.25`, calibrated `h=10.642717`; C3 AUC 0.65 / FZ 0.30; C4 `r=1.5`, 2 windows, occurrence AUC reported as a diagnostic; C6 3 windows, 1,000 bootstrap draws, stability 0.90; confidence gate `tau_conf=0.35` |
| Step 5 gate bars | Recall 0.60 (C1–C6); C0 false-positive rate ≤ 0.10; no row-normalised off-diagonal > 0.40; priority-sensitivity max |Δ macro-F1| ≤ 0.10 |

No threshold or frozen parameter was changed to make a gate pass.

### 2.4 Metrics and gate definitions

- **Recall (gate 5.1):** fraction of SKUs in a cause's panel whose attributed cause
  equals that cause. The C2 gate denominator is `truth_for_gate == "C2"`, which maps
  C2b into C2, giving 400 SKUs. C2b and C2 direct are also reported separately.
  C7 is reported but **not gated** under the accepted six-cause scope.
- **Budget-restricted recall:** the same recall on SKUs where the cause's evidence
  floor was met at least once after `tau_inj` (`evany_*`). Gate 5.1 requires **both**
  the pooled and the budget-restricted recall to clear 0.60 for C1–C6.
- **C0 false-positive rate (gate 5.2):** fraction of C0 SKUs with any non-C0
  attribution at a check at `t >= tau_inj`. This is the strict "any check" reading.
- **Off-diagonal confusion (gate 5.3):** row-normalised confusion over C0–C7; the
  gate fails if any off-diagonal entry exceeds 0.40. C2b is excluded from this
  matrix. The stored gate value for C2 is on the true-C2 row only; a pooled C2 row
  including C2b is reported descriptively in `docs/20` and the results pack.
- **Priority sensitivity (gate 5.4):** 10 random permutations of the attribution
  priority order, compared with the base order by macro-F1 over C1–C7; the gate is
  the maximum absolute difference, bar 0.10.
- **Gate 5.5:** the confusion matrix and statistic distributions were produced.
- **Gate 5.6:** budget-restricted recall is reported. It is a reporting gate, not an
  input to `overall_pass`.
- **Overall pass:** conjunction of 5.1 (pooled **and** budget-restricted), 5.2, 5.3,
  and 5.4.
- **Uncertainty:** the 95% Wilson intervals shown for Step 5 are descriptive,
  computed from the integer counts; the gate bars are applied to point estimates.

### 2.5 Evaluation procedure

The monitor runs from period 24. For each SKU, the **detection reading** is the first
check at `t >= tau_inj = 120` whose attribution is non-C0; if no check after `tau`
is non-C0, the final reading is used. The reported `attributed_cause` is the
attribution at that detection reading. This is the event Step 6's gate also uses.

Consequences:

- a C0 control is a false positive if **any** post-`tau` check names a non-C0 cause;
- a detected cause is scored on the first non-C0 name, not on a later or final
  re-reading;
- undetected SKUs fall back to the final reading.

Step 5 runs the whole 1,800-SKU panel once. There is no train/validation/test split
and no model selection on this panel.

### 3.3 Why Step 5 failed

- **C1 — undetectable injection as generated.** The linear ramp `phi` is too small
  for the bias statistic: the expected `|z_b| ≈ 3.48|phi|` is at most 1.39 even with
  a frozen forecast, below the 1.96 bar. The live adaptive forecaster absorbs the
  rest; its `|z_b| > 1.96` crossing rate was 0.000 on C1 versus 0.067 on C0.
- **C2 — supported on the direct panel.** Recall 0.985, firing 0.990 versus 0.145
  on control (two-proportion z = +17.05); coverage-gap median −0.567 versus 0.000.
- **C2b — corrected generator, still a failed gate.** The correction carries the
  generator's per-period-sigma half-width through panel generation, Step 5, arm (c),
  and inventory, so the aggregate policy interval now carries the intended modelling
  error; demand and the point forecast are untouched. The rebuilt panel's signed
  generator coverage-gap median is −0.176 (absolute median 0.176). The Step 5 monitor
  still evaluates its one-period conformal interval, and C2b attribution recall is
  17/200 = 0.085 [0.054, 0.132]. This is a real interval variant that still misses
  the 0.60 bar, not a detector pass.
- **C3 — undetectable injection as generated.** Median injected `Δp` is +0.031
  (q10 −0.167, q90 +0.188). One sampling sd of an occurrence rate over 36 periods
  near `p=0.2` is about 0.048, and `tau_occ=0.10`; the step is within one standard
  error for most SKUs. The forecaster also tracks the step within about 5 periods.
  The rule fires 0.060 on C3 versus 0.010 on C0 (z = +2.72), but that is 12 SKUs.
- **C4 — specification limitation removed; recall still low.** The injection is
  strong (`k → k × {0.25, 4.0}` at fixed `mu_z`). The correction removed the
  unsatisfiable occurrence-AUC guard from the firing condition; AUC remains a
  diagnostic (C4-panel median 0.435, estimable on 96/200 SKUs). The rule fires on
  15/200 C4 SKUs (0.075 [0.046, 0.120]), so the recall bar is still missed.
- **C5 — detector calibration limitation.** The injection is strong: median `Δp`
  is 0.204 and every C5 SKU clears the generator's detectability budget. The
  correction calibrated the CUSUM threshold on 120 training-control SKUs and 2,880
  pre-injection checks, selecting `h=10.642717` with a measured per-check false-alarm
  rate of 0.04896 against the 0.05 target. On held-out C0 controls the C5 rule fires
  on 10/200 SKUs at any check (0.050 [0.027, 0.090]); the occurrence CUSUM alarms on
  40/200 controls (0.200 [0.150, 0.261]). C5 recall is 7/200 = 0.035 [0.017, 0.070],
  so the verdict remains **FAIL** despite the control calibration behaving as
  targeted.
- **C6 — both generator and detector.** The ADI/CV² cell moves from its initial value
  on 47.1% of SKUs, but the bootstrap-stability guard passes on only 14.2%; the rule
  fires on 0.195 of controls versus 0.200 of C6 SKUs (z = +0.12). Recall is
  33/200 = 0.165.
- **C7 — documented negative.** Recall 57/200 = 0.285, not gated. The policy-outcome
  service statistic separates C7 from control on this synthetic panel (0.310 vs
  0.070, z = +6.12), but this does not reverse the Step 4.9 forecast-diagnostic
  negative; on RUF the cause remains unidentifiable.

**Overall Step 5 verdict: FAIL.** E-8 in `ESCALATIONS.md` remains the authoritative
escalation. This document does not relax or reopen any Step 5 gate.

## 4. Step 6 method

### 4.1 Question and scope

Step 6 asks a narrower question than RQ1:

> When the live monitor makes its first post-injection C2 attribution, can a
> reliability gate decide whether to apply the C2 scale reset, and what happens to
> inventory cost and cycle service level relative to the untouched baseline?

It is **C2-only**, **one decision per SKU**, and conditional on Step 5's C2
attribution. It is not a new attribution test, not a six-cause repair, and not a
test of full CAFR against arm (c).

### 4.2 Panel and split

The same seed-42 panel is used. C2b is excluded everywhere. The split is the
deterministic per-true-cause split used by the reliability screening:

- sort `sku_id` within each true cause;
- cycle `train, train, train, validation, test` (3/1/1);
- each cause therefore contributes 120 train, 40 validation, and 40 test SKUs;
- the test split contains 40 C2 and 40 C0 SKUs.

Only the 40 test C2 and 40 test C0 SKUs enter the inventory comparison. No training
or validation SKU enters the outcome comparison.

The pooled forecaster is still fitted on the first 24 periods of **all 1,800 SKUs**
to reproduce Step 5's live readings. The gate model itself is trained only on
train-split rows.

### 4.3 Reliability gate

The gate is trained only on Step 5 readings satisfying:

- `detected == True`;
- `attributed_cause == "C2"`;
- `true_cause != "C2b"`.

The label is `true_cause == "C2"`. These are the first post-`tau` checkpoint readings,
the exact event at which the policy makes its one decision. Undetected final-reading
rows are excluded.

| Split | Rows | Candidates | True-C2 candidates |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Train | 960 | 246 | 115 |
| Validation | 320 | 75 | 39 |
| Test | 320 | 81 | 37 |

Nine features are taken from the decision reading:

`margin_C2`, `n_fired`, `ev_c2`, `window_full`, `abs_gap`, `neglog10_p`,
`abs_z_b`, `r_strength`, `confidence`.

The model is a pipeline of median imputation, standardisation, and logistic
regression with `max_iter=3000` and `C=1.0`.

**Threshold selection:** the lowest validation threshold with precision ≥ 0.90 and
at least 20 admitted true-C2 validation SKUs. The selected threshold is
**τ = 0.24462755647681794**. On validation it admits 41 rows, 39 of them true C2
(precision 0.951). The test split is never used for selection. If no threshold had
qualified, a rule-only variant would have acted on every detected C2 attribution; it
was not needed.

### 4.4 Policy and C2 remedy

The policy checks at `t >= burn_in = 24`, every `stride = 4` periods, and makes at
most one decision, at the first **non-C0** attribution with `t >= tau_inj = 120`.
The decision rule is:

```
if attributed_cause != C2:            R0, reason not_C2
elif not ev_c2:                       R0, reason insufficient_evidence
elif gate_score < tau:                R0, reason low_reliability
else:                                 R2, reset the C2 scale
```

The remedy is a single assignment:

```
scale(t+1) := 1.0
```

so the order-up-to level becomes

```
S = R * mu_hat + 1.0 * Q_alpha(R-period sums of signed residuals)
```

The point forecast `mu_hat` is not touched. The monitor sees the same scale the
policy uses. This is **not** `apply_r2_conformal`; that function computes an
absolute-residual quantile, which is not the policy's signed aggregate quantile.

Because the safety term is a **signed** residual quantile, `gamma < 1` can move `S`
either up or down. Resetting to 1.0 restores nominal calibration; it is not a
one-way safety-stock increase.

### 4.5 Baseline

The baseline is the same arm (c) on the same demand series, with the C2 scale fixed
at the generator's `gamma` for the whole run, exactly as in Step 5. The gated arm
starts at `gamma` and switches to `1.0` only if the single decision is R2. Both arms
use the same pooled forecaster, simulator configuration, and seed. All comparisons
are paired by SKU.

This baseline isolates the effect of the one-time scale reset. It is **not** an
accuracy-based-selection comparator; both arms already use arm (c).

### 4.6 Timing, evaluation window, and lead time

- A decision is made **after** the level for period `t` is computed and the monitor
  is updated with `y_t`. The new scale is first used by the call at `t + 1`.
- The order placed at the end of `t + 1` arrives at the start of `t + 1 + L`, with
  `L = 2`. There is no look-ahead.
- The outcome window was fixed from the **training** split only:
  `EVAL_START = tau_inj + 12 = 132`; evaluated periods **[132, 200) = 68**.
- Of 120 detected train C2 SKUs, **95.8%** have
  `detect_t + 1 + lead_time <= 132`; the 5 late detectors are reported. In the
  comparison, all 40 C2 decisions occurred at `t = 120` or `t = 124`; actions began
  at 121 or 125; the first affected orders arrived at 123 or 127, before 132.
- The 68-period window contains **22 complete 3-period cycles** (66 periods). The
  last two periods contribute to cost and period service level but not to cycle CSL.

### 4.7 Outcomes and inference

- **Cost:** per-SKU sum of `cost_period` over [132, 200), where
  `cost_period = H * on_hand_end + B * unmet`, `B/H = 19`, and `H` is the per-SKU
  holding rate. The base config's `H=1.0` is overridden by each SKU's recorded
  holding rate.
- **Cycle CSL:** fraction of complete 3-period cycles with zero lost demand,
  averaged over SKUs.
- **Period service level:** fraction of evaluated periods with zero lost demand.
  Secondary; no bootstrap interval is stored.
- **Paired difference:** gated minus baseline for the same SKU, then averaged.
- **95% interval:** percentile bootstrap over SKUs, 10,000 resamples, seed 42,
  percentiles 2.5/97.5, computed in `paired_bootstrap`.

The two outcomes are reported without multiplicity adjustment. This is an
exploratory feasibility analysis, not a confirmatory test.

bootstrap interval for period SL is stored, so no interval is reported.

Direction of the C2 effect, per SKU: cost rose on 21, fell on 16, and was unchanged
on 3; cycle CSL rose on 27, fell on 0, and was unchanged on 13. The mixed cost
direction is consistent with resetting a signed quantile. CSL did not fall on any C2
SKU in this seed.

### 5.4 Interpretation

In this seed-42 feasibility experiment, **service improved in the C2 group**
(+6.14 pp cycle CSL; interval entirely above zero) and **cost increased by 2.85%**
(+71.48 on 2507.26; interval includes zero). The C0 group is exactly unchanged on all
40 SKUs. The overall 80-SKU cost interval also includes zero. **No cost saving is
demonstrated.** The 95% service target is not reached: gated C2 CSL is 0.8625 and C0
CSL is 0.8818.

The result is consistent with the remedy restoring the nominal C2 scale without
changing the point forecast. It does not establish a cost reduction, generalisation
beyond this seed and panel, real-world validity, or publication novelty.

## 6. Limitations and threats to validity

1. **Synthetic only.** Every result is on a generated intermittent-demand panel. No
   RUF, M5, or other real dataset was used. There is no real-world validation and no
   demonstrated transfer.
2. **Single seed.** The panel and both experiments use seed 42. The generator's
   held-out seed 43 was not used. No multi-seed replication or seed-level uncertainty
   is reported.
3. **Small test set.** Step 6 uses 40 C2 + 40 C0 test SKUs, with 22 complete cycles
   per SKU. The bootstrap resamples 40 C2 SKUs, so its intervals are coarse. This is a
   feasibility result, not a confirmation.
4. **No demonstrated cost reduction.** The C2 point estimate is a 2.85% cost
   increase, and the 95% interval includes zero. The all-80 estimate is +1.39%, also
   with an interval that includes zero. No cost saving, cost neutrality, or
   cost-effectiveness claim is supported.
5. **Service is below the 95% target.** Gated C2 cycle CSL is 0.8625; C0 is 0.8818.
   The scale reset repairs the C2-specific miscalibration only; a residual service gap
   is shared with the control and is not repaired.
6. **Pooled-forecaster exposure and possible leakage.** The global pooled forecaster
   is fitted on the first 24 periods of all 1,800 SKUs, including the 40 C2 and 40 C0
   test SKUs. The gate model is trained only on train-split rows, but the forecaster's
   burn-in exposure to test SKUs is mild leakage. Its effect on gate robustness is not
   measured. A leak-free replication would fit the pool on train SKUs only or freeze
   it at burn-in.
7. **Step 5 attribution is largely unvalidated.** Only C2 direct passes the recall
   bar (0.985, 197/200). C1 and C3 are undetectable as generated; C4's unsatisfiable
   guard was removed but recall is 0.075; C5's CUSUM was recalibrated but recall is
   0.035; C6 fails on both generator and detector; C7 is a documented negative. The
   C2b generator now carries its interval error into the policy, but its recall is
   0.085, so it also fails. No six-cause attribution claim is possible.
8. **C2-only, one-time remedy.** Step 6 repairs one C2 scale, once per SKU. It does
   not adapt over time, does not address C2b or any other cause, and does not test a
   multi-cause policy. The result is conditional on C2 attribution.
9. **No full-CAFR or churn comparison.** Both arms use arm (c). The bandit, churn
   control, and the original full-CAFR-versus-accuracy-selection hypothesis were not
   run. Step 6 isolates a C2 scale reset, not CAFR's full decision layer.
10. **Gate limitations.** The threshold was selected on 75 validation candidates;
    the test split has 81 candidates. All-cause test precision is 0.822, while the
    C2+C0 comparison precision is 0.974; the difference is seven false actions on
    C1–C7 SKUs outside the comparison. The gate missed three true C2 SKUs through
    attribution, and six of seven false C2 attributions on controls were filtered as
    low reliability. The 0.974 figure must not be quoted as full-panel precision.
11. **Inference limitations.** Two outcomes are reported without multiplicity
    adjustment. Period SL has no stored interval. Cycle CSL is based on 22 cycles and
    is noisy. The bootstrap is over SKUs, not over seeds or generator cells.
12. **Single cost model.** Cost is holding plus lost-sales penalty, with no fixed
    ordering cost and one synthetic DGP. `H` varies by SKU and `B/H=19`; the results
    are conditional on that simulator.
13. **C2 injection weakness.** Seven of 200 C2 SKUs cannot reach the 0.12 margin at
    the 0.02 gamma floor; four are in [0.10, 0.12) and three are below 0.10. Two of
    the seven are in the 40-SKU test group (both in [0.10, 0.12)). Those injections
    are weaker than the constraint intends.
14. **Different coverage objects.** The generator solves `gamma` at 95% coverage;
    the monitor checks a 90% conformal interval. They are related by the same `gamma`
    but are not the same statistic, and this document does not equate them.
15. **Historical tests.** The 186 passing tests recorded in `docs/19` were not
    rerun for the results pack or this document. They are historical evidence, not a
    fresh test result.

## 8. Claims we must not make

The following claims are not supported by the executed experiments:

- **“Full CAFR beats accuracy-based selection at equal churn.”** Not tested; the
  original claim is retired.
- **“CAFR reduces inventory cost.”** The C2 cost point estimate is +2.85%, with a 95%
  interval that includes zero; the all-80 estimate is +1.39%, also including zero.
- **“The C2 repair is cost-neutral or cost-saving.”** Neither is demonstrated.
- **“The result generalises beyond this seed and panel.”** Single seed, synthetic
  panel, 40 C2 + 40 C0 test SKUs.
- **“The 95% service target is met.”** Gated C2 CSL is 0.8625; C0 is 0.8818.
- **“The attribution pipeline detects six causes.”** Step 5 fails overall; only C2
  direct passes its recall bar. C1, C3, C4, C5, C6, and C7 are negatives.
- **“The C2b detector works.”** The C2b panel now carries the interval error, but
  recall is 0.085; the detector does not clear the bar.
- **“The reliability gate is a validated general classifier.”** It is a seed-42
  feasibility gate; all-cause test precision is 0.822, not 0.974.
- **“0.974 is the full-panel precision.”** It is the precision on the 80-SKU C2+C0
  comparison only.
- **“Period service-level improvement is statistically significant.”** No interval
  is stored for period SL; only point estimates are available.
- **“Step 6 tests full CAFR, the bandit, or churn control.”** It tests a one-time
  C2 scale reset on arm (c).
- **“The results establish publication novelty.”** No novelty claim is made or
  established here; no literature search was part of this task.
- **“The cost increase is statistically significant.”** The C2 cost interval
  includes zero.

## 9. Unresolved discrepancies and reporting notes

These are source-artifact labels, orientations, or denominator choices. They are
recorded rather than silently corrected. No source, configuration, or result file was
modified.

1. **`step5_summary.json` confusion keys.** `confusion` and
   `confusion_rownormalised` are stored in a column-oriented/mislabeled form and do
   not match the row-normalised table in `docs/20`. The confusion matrix in
   `step5_confusion.parquet` and the recomputation from
   `step5_readings.parquet` are row = truth, column = attributed. The recomputed
   values match `docs/20`.
2. **`step5_confusion.parquet` excludes C2b.** It is 8×8 (C0–C7). C2b counts and the
   C2 pooled denominator come from `step5_readings.parquet`.
3. **Step 5 recall intervals are not stored.** `step5_summary.json` stores the C2
   pooled recall and C2b recall but not the C2 direct count or the Wilson intervals.
   The intervals in §3.1 are computed from the integer counts and match the rounded
   values in `docs/20`.
4. **Step 5 “final reading” shorthand.** The results pack captions describe the
   recall as being measured at the final reading. The implementation instead uses the
   first post-`tau` non-C0 attribution; only undetected SKUs fall back to the final
   reading. The counts in §3.1 are the implementation's counts. This is a caption
   imprecision, not a numerical discrepancy.
5. **Step 5 gate 5.3 C2 row.** The stored gate value for C2 is 0.005 on the true-C2
   row. `docs/20` and the results pack also report 0.238 for a pooled C2 row that
   includes C2b; both are below the 0.40 bar. The gate itself used the true-C2 value.
6. **Step 5 budget-restricted denominators.** Gate 5.1's budget-restricted recall
   uses evidence availability (`evany_*`): C4 n=39, C5 n=68. `docs/20` and E-8
   additionally report the generator's budget-flag denominators: C4 n=72, C5 n=200.
   Both sets fail the 0.60 bar; they are not the same denominator and should not be
   compared directly.
7. **Period service-level intervals are absent.** `c2_compare_summary.json` stores
   cost and CSL bootstrap intervals but no period-SL interval. Only point estimates
   are reported here.
8. **Step 6 `elapsed_s` is a suspend artifact.** The comparison JSON's 10,833.3 s is
   wall-clock time affected by machine suspend. The progress log shows 70/80 SKUs at
   118.4 s; re-timing the last selected C0 SKU took 0.73 s for both arms; the clean
   20-SKU timing is 13.53 s. Do not cite 10,833.3 s as compute time.
9. **Step 6 `live_actions_by_cause` label.** The summary key counts `attributed`
   values on the gated arm (C2 44, C6 7, C5 5, C1 1, C7 1), not R2 actions. The 80-SKU
   comparison has 38 R2 actions. Use `c2_compare_arms.csv` for action counts.
10. **Two test precisions are intentional.** All-cause test precision is 0.822
    (45 acted; 37 true C2; 8 non-C2: one C0 + seven C1–C7). C2+C0 comparison precision
    is 0.974 (38 acted; 37 true C2; one C0). They are not interchangeable.
11. **Tests were not rerun.** `docs/19` records 186 passing tests in 63.57 s. The
    results pack and this document read artifacts only and do not depend on a new test
    run.

No numerical value in this document was estimated or invented. Values were read from
the listed artifacts or recomputed directly from stored counts; the remaining
discrepancies are the labels/orientations/denominators above.
