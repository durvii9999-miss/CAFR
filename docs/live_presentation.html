Problem Motivation: Why Intermittent Forecasting is Difficult

In spare-parts logistics (aerospace, heavy machinery, defence), demand series consist of prolonged sequences of zero demand interrupted by sporadic, high-variance requirements. Standard statistical and machine-learning models encounter three distinct failure modes:

  • Symmetric Loss Mismatch: Mean Squared Error (MSE) penalizes positive and negative errors equally. In inventory control, an under-prediction leads to stockouts and equipment downtime, whereas an over-prediction incurs manageable unit holding costs.
  • Phantom Fractional Predictions: When trained on panels with 80% to 90% zeros, pooled gradient-boosted trees and deep networks predict small non-zero decimals (e.g., 0.3 to 0.8 units) continuously across inactive periods, accumulating substantial upward cumulative bias.
  • The Zero-Baseline Distortion: Naively forecasting zero yields strong statistical point metrics on sparse panels, but carries systematic negative bias and fails to inform stocking buffers for positive demand occurrences.

Research Question & Investigated Gap

The Research Question: Rather than discarding forecasters or retraining computationally expensive global models when tracking errors spike, can post-forecast monitoring attribute failures to specific root causes (e.g., interval miscalibration, intermittency shifts, obsolescence), and can targeted closed-form policy remedies restore downstream inventory service?

Investigated Gap: Current state-of-the-art methods either rely on global model retraining (which risks catastrophic forgetting on intermittent series) or apply blanket safety-stock inflation. CAFR investigates cause-attributed repair: diagnosing the structural failure mechanism and executing targeted intervention rules.

Research Focus, Novelty Scope & Study Distinction

Research Focus: Measuring when cause-attributed repair of intermittent-demand forecasts is identifiable and when it is not.
What Distinguishes This Study

The emphasis is on measuring the limits and operational implications of cause-attributed repair, rather than assuming that a detected change can always be correctly diagnosed and successfully repaired.

Scope of Novelty Claim

A candidate methodological contribution requiring comparison with closely related literature; not a claim that no prior study has addressed these problems.

SBA vs. Global LightGBM Comparison

Global LightGBM exhibits 2.99× higher mean sMAE than SBA (12.7423 vs 4.2642), a statistically significant difference (paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test $p = 2.57 \times 10^{-58}$). Because L2 loss penalizes large errors heavily, the pooled tree fits residual positive numbers (0.3–0.8) across long zero sequences, creating +1.8038 mean bias and accumulating +3,179.86 units of cumulative phantom demand (PIS).

Understanding the Zero Baseline Metric

The Zero baseline achieves mean sMAE 2.6697 and median 1.0000 because for the median SKU, evaluation demand equals burn-in demand ($\bar{y}_{\text{eval}}/\bar{y}_{\text{burn}} = 1.0$). However, forecasting zero yields systematic negative bias (-1.4430 units/month) and WAPE of 1.0000 (100% relative error), providing no positive buffer for real demand occurrences.

Scope Boundary Note: The RAF panel serves strictly as an external real-world forecasting benchmark. Because historical logistics logs lack ground-truth causal labels, RAF does not validate CAFR's cause attribution or repair mechanisms.

Three Contributions Investigated

  • 1. Identifiability Analysis: Evaluate cause-specific detection under controlled demand-change scenarios and quantify attribution errors across the 7-cause taxonomy.
  • 2. Decision-to-Impact Evaluation: Examine how detection and repair decisions relate to downstream inventory service level and cost outcomes (Cycle Service Level α, fill rate, holding and shortage costs).
  • 3. Two-Setting Evaluation: Use labelled synthetic experiments to study cause attribution where ground truth is known, and a separate historical RAF dataset to evaluate forecasting behaviour on real demand histories.

Evidence Status & Scope of Novelty

Evidence Status: Initial results support further investigation (verified 98.5% recall on direct C2 and +6.14 pp service recovery in Step 6 feasibility). However, several detection acceptance criteria remain unmet (40.5% C0 false positives, sub-60% recall on C1/C3–C6), and the equal-churn operational comparison remains incomplete.

Scope of Novelty Claim: A candidate methodological contribution requiring comparison with closely related literature; not a claim that no prior study has addressed these problems.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Why not simply retrain LightGBM or use an ensemble when errors spike?

Retraining global models across thousands of intermittent series is computationally heavy and risks overfitting to sporadic recent bursts. Our RAF empirical benchmark shows that standard pooled LightGBM produces 2.99× higher error than SBA due to L2-driven phantom demand. CAFR's principle is diagnostic: identify whether the shift is in demand size, arrival probability, or interval scale, and apply a closed-form correction.

Q2: What is the current verification status of CAFR's attribution engine?

On single-cause interval miscalibration ($C_2$), the detector achieves verified 98.5% recall (197/200 SKUs). However, on the broader multi-cause panel, Step 5 overall failed its pre-registered acceptance criteria due to a 40.5% false-alarm rate on $C_0$ controls and low recall on $C_1$ and $C_3\text{--}C_6$. We are currently testing multi-checkpoint confirmation (dwell latching) to suppress control false positives.

Q3: Did Step 6 demonstrate cost savings in inventory management?

No, and we do not claim cost savings. Step 6 demonstrated significant service recovery (+6.14 percentage points in CSL), but downstream cost change (+71.48) has a 95% confidence interval spanning zero ([-20.01, 168.63]). Restoring an underestimated safety buffer naturally incurs additional unit inventory holding cost.

Q4: What does the RAF historical evaluation actually prove?

RAF provides an independent empirical stress test on 5,000 aircraft spare parts with 89.83% zeros. It proves that classical intermittent methods (SBA, TSB) maintain robust tracking error on extreme sparsity, whereas generic pooled regression trees inflate prediction error by nearly 200%. It does not claim to validate CAFR's cause attribution, because historical logs lack causal ground-truth labels.

Q5: What is the exact scope of novelty, and what distinguishes this research?

Our research focus is measuring when cause-attributed repair of intermittent-demand forecasts is identifiable and when it is not. What distinguishes this study from prior literature is that we do not take diagnosis for granted: the emphasis is on measuring the limits and operational implications of cause-attributed repair, rather than assuming that a detected change can always be correctly diagnosed and successfully repaired.
