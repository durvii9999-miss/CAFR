**Do not paraphrase anything in this section.** These are the strongest claims the project is entitled to make. Do not inflate them and do not soften them. (Rule 11 in file `14` §14.)

### 4.1 The gap — the four-part seam (Rev 2 §2.1, as stated in file 14 §1.4)

> No work was found that does **all four** of these together:
>
> 1. Classify **why an intermittent-demand forecast failed**, by mechanism.
> 2. Map each mechanism to a **matched remedy, including non-forecast actions** (recalibrate the interval, adjust the policy, or do nothing).
> 3. **Learn** that mapping from **downstream inventory service/cost feedback** — not from accuracy.
> 4. **Explicitly control model/action churn** by design.

### 4.2 The contribution — one sentence (verbatim from file `08` §E)

> **We introduce a cause-attributed forecast-repair layer for intermittent spare-parts demand that classifies each forecast failure by mechanism — separating occurrence failure, size failure, level shift, intermittency change, uncertainty mis-calibration, and inventory-policy mis-set — and selects the matched corrective action, where the cause→remedy mapping is learned from downstream inventory service-level feedback rather than from forecast accuracy.**

**The falsifiable claim (the paper's centrepiece) — quote it exactly:**

> *At equal model churn, cause-attributed repair beats accuracy-based model selection on the inventory cost–service frontier.*

## 5. READY-TO-ADAPT DRAFT TEXT

### 5.1 Draft Introduction — finished prose you can adapt

> **The problem.** Industrial spare-parts planners hold stock so that a machine can be repaired when it fails. Holding too much stock ties up money and floor space; holding too little means a stockout and a machine that waits. Both costs are real, and the planner's job is to sit between them.
>
> Spare-parts demand is not like retail demand. It is **intermittent**: most months most parts sell nothing at all. In the panel we use, **70.08 % of SKU-months have zero demand**, and the median part has only **12 nonzero months out of 60**. When demand does arrive it arrives in irregular, sometimes large lumps. A forecaster cannot learn much from a series with twelve nonzero points.
>
> **Why the standard practice is not enough.** The standard practice for this setting is to keep several forecasting models in play and to switch to whichever one currently scores best on an accuracy metric such as MASE. Three things are wrong with that.
>
> First, **accuracy does not predict service.** A decision-aware benchmark across 38 forecasting methods reports a Spearman correlation of **−0.555** between a method's accuracy rank and the contractual service level it actually achieves [5]. Choosing the most accurate model can make service worse.
>
> Second, **it never asks why.** When a forecast fails, this practice changes the model. It never diagnoses the mechanism. A service failure caused by a mis-set safety stock is "repaired" by changing the forecaster, which cannot possibly help — the forecast was never the problem.
>
> Third, **it churns.** Continuous re-selection switches the active model constantly. The cost of that churn is neither measured nor constrained.
>
> **The gap.** No work we could find does all four of these things together: it classifies **why** an intermittent-demand forecast failed, by mechanism; it maps each mechanism to a **matched remedy — including remedies that never touch the forecast**; it **learns** that mapping from **inventory service and cost**, not from accuracy; and it **controls churn by design**. Each of the nearest works holds one or two of these. None holds all four. [Insert the honest scoping sentence from §4.4 here.]
>
> **What this paper does.** We describe CAFR, a cause-attributed forecast-repair layer. It runs in four stages. It forecasts with a standard pool of intermittent-demand methods. It monitors the outcome and attributes the failure to one of seven mechanisms, or abstains. It holds a library of remedies, one matched to each mechanism, three of which do not touch the forecast at all. And it selects the remedy with a contextual bandit whose reward is realised inventory cost and service — never forecast accuracy.
>
> **The claim, stated in advance.** At equal model churn, cause-attributed repair beats accuracy-based model selection on the inventory cost–service frontier. **If the experiment does not show this, the claim fails and this paper says so.** We fix the success criteria before the test run.

#### 5.3.1 Problem setup

We study a single-echelon, single-location spare-parts inventory under **periodic review with an order-up-to policy**, denoted `(S, S)`: at the end of each review period `t` the planner observes the inventory position and places one order that raises it to a level `S_{i,t}` for SKU `i`.

The review period is one month. The replenishment **lead time is `L = 2` periods**, so the **protection interval is `R = L + 1 = 3` periods**: an order placed at the end of `t` arrives at the start of `t + L`, and the level must cover demand over `R` periods. `L = 2` and `R = 3` are held identical across every SKU and every arm. We sweep `L ∈ {1, 2, 4}` in the sensitivity analysis only.

Demand is intermittent: a period may be a zero-demand period. Unmet demand is treated as **[backordered / lost — state which model is used, per Rev 2 §15.6 step 4]**. Costs are `H` per unit held per period and `B` per unit backordered per period. We sweep the cost ratio **`B/H ∈ {4, 9, 19, 49}`**, which under a newsvendor reading corresponds to service targets of roughly 80 %, 90 %, 95 % and 98 %. We report the **cost–service frontier** — the (cost, achieved CSL) locus traced out by sweeping `B/H` and the target service level — **not a single (cost, CSL) point per arm.** A single point is not interpretable, because two arms can be compared at different operating points and the comparison is then meaningless.

**Order-up-to level.** The level is the **empirical α-quantile of the `R`-period aggregate demand**, not a Gaussian construction:

```
S_{i,t} = Q̂_α(i, t)   =   R·μ̂_{i,t} + F̂⁻¹(α)
```

where `F̂` is the empirical CDF of the **`R`-period aggregate** residual `A^{agg}_{i,t} − R·μ̂_{i,t}` over the trailing window. The Gaussian `z` is deliberately dropped even when `σ̂` is well estimated, because intermittent-demand errors are right-skewed. The quantile is floored at `α_max = min(0.999, 1 − 1/(n+1))`, where `n` is the number of nonzero cycles available — the controller may never demand a quantile the data cannot estimate. [State the reason explicitly: a per-period standard deviation applied to an `R`-period protection interval understates safety stock by 42.3 % at `R = 3`, which would have made every arm miss its service target for a reason unrelated to any hypothesis. This is Rev 2 §15.6 and it is worth one sentence in the paper — it is a methodological contribution in its own right.]

#### 5.3.2 The pipeline, in four stages

CAFR is a closed loop with **one decision point per SKU per review period**. The stages are:

**Stage 1 — Forecast (M0).** A pool of intermittent-demand forecasters: **Croston, SBA, TSB, mSBA, mTSB, SES-on-nonzero-sizes**, and one **global LightGBM** pooled across all SKUs with SKU static features and lagged demand. Each produces a point forecast and a set of quantiles. Every method reads its fitting input through a single configurable switch `fit_on` (§5.3.7), fixed identically across all arms.

**Stage 2 — Diagnose (M1 + M2).** The outcome monitor (M1) computes, per SKU per period: rolling residuals; bias separately for the occurrence sub-process and the size sub-process; empirical prediction-interval coverage; ADI and CV²; **two CUSUM statistics, one per sub-process**; and the realised inventory outcome (achieved CSL, fill rate, backorders, PIS, NOS). The attribution module (M2) maps this feature vector to **one of seven causes, or to an abstain**, plus a confidence score. **The rules are implemented first and are the paper's method**; an optional classifier over the same features is evaluated *against* the rules, never instead of them.

**Stage 3 — Repair (M3).** A library of remedies, one matched to each cause, plus an explicit no-action. Three of the eight remedies never touch the point forecast.

**Stage 4 — Select and learn (M4 + M5).** The selection policy is a **Thompson-sampling contextual bandit with a linear contextual model**, one global policy across all SKUs, generalising through a context vector. The reward is realised inventory cost and service (§5.3.6). The simulator (M5) advances one period with the action in force, and the realised outcome becomes the reward.

**Timing, and the one mandatory delay.** At the end of review period `t` for SKU `i`: M1 emits the feature vector → M2 emits (cause, confidence) → M4 selects a remedy or abstains → the action is applied → M5 advances one period → the realised cost/service becomes the reward → the posterior updates. **One period of delay is mandatory.** The reward must be the outcome of the action, never of a period the action did not affect.

**No look-ahead.** At the decision point for period `t`, the monitor may read only data up to and including `t`. The simulator's true demand, the ground-truth cause labels, and any statistic computed over the full series are unreachable by the policy — enforced by an **exact column whitelist with set equality**, not by a name blacklist, and additionally by a file-level separation in which the policy loader's signature cannot return the ground-truth frame at all. **[Do not write the whitelist test as a blacklist in the paper's description. Rev 1's name blacklist missed `cause_active` and `injection_param`, the two columns that give the answer away, and it failed open.]**

#### 5.3.3 The seven causes, and the abstain

A cause is a **hypothesis about the mechanism** that produced the observed error. It is not a description of the error. Two causes can produce similar-looking errors; that overlap is the substance of RQ1, not a defect.

| ID | Cause | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **C1** | Persistent directional bias | The forecaster is systematically wrong in one direction, **stable and gradual**, in the **size** sub-process. |
| **C2** | Uncertainty mis-calibration | The point forecast is acceptable but the **predictive interval** is wrong: empirical coverage of the nominal α-interval is persistently off α. |
| **C3** | Occurrence failure | The forecaster misjudges **whether** demand occurs — occurrence AUC low, or false-zero rate high — while conditional sizes are fine. |
| **C4** | Size failure | Occurrence is predicted acceptably, but the **nonzero size** is mis-modelled: error **dispersion** on nonzero periods is high and growing relative to the SKU's own baseline, **at an unchanged mean**. |
| **C5** | Level / regime shift | An **abrupt** change in the data-generating process — a step in level or in occurrence rate — that the estimator has not absorbed because it still averages over pre-shift data. |
| **C6** | Intermittency change | The SKU's **intermittency cell** has crossed a boundary and stayed there, so the method family in use is no longer appropriate. |
| **C7** | Inventory-policy mis-set | The forecast is **acceptable** by every other test, yet achieved service persistently misses target. The fault is in the policy parameters, not the forecast. |
| **C0** | No actionable signal | *(Not a cause — the abstain.)* Confidence below threshold. **Do nothing.** A first-class action, not a fallback. |

**Two definitional points to state in the paper, because a reader will otherwise read them as errors.**
1. **C1 does not require bias in both sub-processes.** Occurrence bias is computed and reported but is not a gate. Requiring it would make C1 undetectable under the injected mechanism, which ramps the size mean at a fixed occurrence rate.
2. **C6's boundary is `ADI* = 4.0`, not the textbook 1.32.** A spare-parts panel has `ADI ≥ 2` by construction (`ADI = 1/p` with `p ≤ 0.50`), so 1.32 can never be crossed. The name and meaning of C6 are unchanged; only the numerical boundary moves, from one that cannot be crossed to one that can.

**The detection statistics** are computed over eight configurable trailing horizons: bias over `W_short = 24` with a sign-stability window of `4 × W_short = 96`; coverage over `W_cal = 30`; occurrence AUC over `W_occ = [36 — see the panel note in §5.3.8; Rev 2 specifies 60, which does not fit a 60-period panel]`; size dispersion over `W_sz = 24`; the ADI/CV² cell over `W_class = 30`; the service gap over `W_svc = 30`. CUSUM parameters are `k = 0.25` and `h = 4.0` per sub-process. **These are not free parameters: `k` and `h` were co-designed with the injected effect magnitudes through a detectability budget, and tuning them after generating the panel silently breaks that budget.**

**[Optional paragraph — the detectability budget.** Detection delay is a function of intermittency. A cause can only be detected once enough **nonzero** periods have accumulated, and the median SKU in our real panel has **12 nonzero periods in 60 months**. Detection delay is therefore reported as a **descriptive property of intermittent demand**, per cause, against the budget line — not as a defect of the detectors. This pre-empts a reviewer reading a low recall on one cause as a method failure.**]**

#### 5.3.4 The routing rule

Attribution is a **priority-ordered rule set**, evaluated in a fixed order:

```
C-guard  →  C5 (abrupt shift)  →  C1 (gradual bias)  →  C4 (size dispersion)
         →  C2 (calibration, mean OK)  →  C3 (occurrence)  →  C6 (cell change)
         →  C7 (policy, all else clear)  →  C0 (abstain)
```

**Two ordering choices need a sentence each, because both are deliberate.**
- **C4 precedes C2.** A change in the size distribution necessarily moves coverage, so C2 would otherwise shadow C4 on every C4 series. C4's signature (dispersion at a fixed mean) is more specific and must be tested first.
- **C5 comes first.** An abrupt shift invalidates the trailing statistics that every other detector depends on, so it must be resolved before anything else fires.

Each detector fires only when its own evidence floor is met — otherwise it abstains. The floors are: **≥ 8 nonzero periods** for C1; **≥ 10** for C3 and C4; **≥ 30 coverage observations** for C2; **≥ 20 usable periods and ≥ 2 unmet review cycles** for C7.

**Confidence and abstention.** Each fired cause carries a confidence score that is **monotone in evidence strength** and **decreases when several causes fire together**. A cause is only acted on when `conf ≥ τ_conf`, with `τ_conf = 0.35`. Below that threshold — or when the evidence floor is not met — the system abstains (C0).

**[Say this ordering explicitly: `τ_conf` was tuned on the training split, its value was fixed on the validation split, and the test panel was used for confirmation only. The ≤ 10 % false-positive rate on the control panel is a design target that `τ_conf` was tuned to, and is reported as a design characteristic, not as an independent result.]**

**A censoring guard runs before every statistic.** A period is flagged as potentially censored when the inventory position reached zero during it. All bias, CUSUM and size-error statistics are computed only over classified-uncensored periods. Structural zeros — genuine no-demand periods — are **not** censored and are never discarded. The censoring guard does **not** cover the forecaster's own training input; that is handled separately by `fit_on` (§5.3.7).

#### 5.3.5 The remedies

One matched remedy per cause, drawn from a single library of eight. **The cause that was attributed does not by itself determine which remedy is applied — that is the selection policy's job (§5.3.6).**

| Cause | Remedy | Action |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **R1** Damped intercept correction | Add `λ·b` to the point forecast, where `b` is the recent mean residual, with the occurrence and size components corrected **separately**. Default `λ = 1.0` for occurrence and `λ = 0.5` for size, because size residuals are noisier. Continuous — not a model switch. |
| C2 | **R2** Conformal recalibration | Recompute the empirical quantiles of the aggregate residual over the last `W_cal = 24` periods and rebuild the interval. Widens or narrows. **Non-forecast: the point forecast is untouched.** |
| C3 | **R3** Switch the occurrence sub-model | Move to a probability-based occurrence model (TSB, or a hurdle classifier). **Size model untouched.** |
| C4 | **R4** Re-estimate the size model | Refit on nonzero sizes only with recency weighting. **Occurrence model untouched.** |
| C5 | **R5** Discard pre-shift data, add a step indicator | Set the estimation window to `[τ, t]` at the detected change point, re-initialise there, and add a step term so the estimator does not smooth across the break. |
| C6 | **R6** Re-classify and re-initialise | Recompute the (ADI, CV²) cell and re-map to a method family through a fixed, documented mapping table. |
| C7 | **R7** **Adjust the safety factor — leave the forecast alone** | The service-probability controller below. |
| C0 | **R0** Do nothing | Keep the current forecaster and policy unchanged. |

**The C7 controller, stated exactly.** Let `α_t` be the service probability the policy currently targets and `ĉ_t` the achieved CSL over `W_svc`. Then

```
α_{t+1} = clip( α_t + κ · ( α* − ĉ_t ) ,  α_min ,  α_max )

with  α_min = 0.50,  α_max = min( 0.999 , 1 − 1/(n_t + 1) ),  κ = 1.0   (n_t = nonzero cycles available)

and the order-up-to level is  S_{i,t} = Q̂_{α_t}(i, t).
```

`κ`'s units are **service-probability per unit of CSL gap**. `α_t` is directly readable by a practitioner — "the policy is currently targeting the 94th percentile". **R7 must not touch the point forecast.** If R7 is implemented as "change the forecast", the paper's central distinction collapses.

**Why the remedies matter even though none of them is new.** Interval widening, bias correction, safety-stock adjustment and TSB all exist off the shelf [22][28]. **We do not invent the remedies — we invent the selection of them.** This is a strength, and it moves the novelty claim to where it actually lives.

**No action is a first-class action.** R0 is selected when the evidence floor is not met, when confidence is below `τ_conf`, when no cause fires and service is on target, when two or more causes fire at comparable strength, or when a dwell-time constraint is active. It is the mechanism by which CAFR controls churn **by design** rather than by post-hoc thresholding.

#### 5.3.6 The reward, and the selection policy

**The reward is realised inventory cost and service. It is never forecast error.**

```
r_{i,t} = − [ ( H · Ī_{i,t+1} + B · B̄_{i,t+1} ) − C̄_i ] / σ_{C,i}

with the σ-floor:   σ_{C,i} ← max( σ_{C,i} , 0.05 · C̄_i )
```

`C̄_i` and `σ_{C,i}` are the SKU's burn-in mean and standard deviation of period cost, computed on the simulator before any arm is scored. **Two clauses are mandatory and each needs a sentence in the paper.**
1. **The per-SKU standardisation is mandatory.** Raw cost scales with SKU volume by orders of magnitude; an unstandardised reward makes the bandit a policy for the three largest SKUs and noise for the rest.
2. **The σ-floor is mandatory.** Without it the reward explodes for any SKU with near-constant cost, and those updates dominate the bandit.

**Why cost/service and not accuracy — state it as the paper's clearest line.** PtC's reward is `r_t = τ(e_ML,t − e_CB,t)`, a forecast-error reward. Ours is not forecast error, not MASE, not RMSSE. **Ablation A5** replaces our reward with a standardised accuracy reward `−|e_t| / σ_{e,i}` using the **same σ-floor**, in the **same action space** — so the only difference is the reward's *content*.

**The context vector** is `x_{i,t} = [ x^inv_{i,t} , x^det_{i,t} ]`, assembled from M1 features only, all observable at decision time, and standardised using training-split statistics only, then frozen. The blocks are: the attributed cause (one-hot) and its confidence; error features (rolling bias, size dispersion, occurrence AUC, false-zero rate, coverage); intermittency features (ADI, CV², cell, mean nonzero size, zero fraction); inventory features (CSL gap, fill rate, average on-hand, average backorders, PIS, NOS); control features (periods since last switch, active remedy); and SKU statics (lead time, unit cost, holding rate, demand class). [The full feature list and the standardisation constants go in **T7**.]

**Forgetting is required, not optional.** C5 and C6 *are* non-stationarity injections: they are the causes defined by the world changing. A standard Thompson sampler's posterior precision grows with every observation, so its variance collapses and it **stops exploring exactly when the world has changed** — CAFR would systematically under-perform on the causes it exists to handle. We therefore inflate the affected SKU's posterior variance on a CUSUM alarm, or discard the last `W_detect` observations from its sufficient statistics. This ties the learner's adaptation to the diagnosis: knowing the cause does not only select the remedy, it also tells the learner to forget. **[The forgetting mechanism is identical on arms (f), (f′) and A4. This is a hard requirement — if A4 adapts at a different speed, the H1′ comparison measures adaptation speed rather than attribution. State it, and note it is unit-tested.]**

#### 5.3.7 Churn: definition, measurement and control

At period `t` for SKU `i` the active **configuration** is the pair `(base_forecaster_family, active_remedy)`. We report two churn metrics separately, because they are not the same phenomenon:

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Model churn** `χ^model` | Fraction of periods where the **base forecaster family** changed. **The strict metric.** R1, R2 and R7 do not cause model churn — they are parameter adjustments. R3, R4, R5 and R6 do. |
| **Action churn** `χ^action` | Fraction of periods where the **active remedy** changed. Includes parameter adjustments. |

**The headline comparison uses `χ^model`**, because that is what "model selection" means in arm (c) and it is the metric the equal-churn constraint is imposed on. Both are reported. We also report the **distribution** of inter-switch intervals, not only the mean — a mean hides bimodality, and some SKUs never switch while others oscillate.

**Forced-exploration decisions are excluded from every churn count.** A decision the policy did not choose is not a switch.

**Three control mechanisms, in order of strength:**
1. **R0 abstain** — low-confidence cases consume no switch. This is the **design** mechanism.
2. **Dwell time `d = 3`** — after any action at `t`, no further switch until `t + d`. Exception: a CUSUM alarm overrides the dwell, because a genuine level shift must be absorbed immediately. Every override is logged.
3. **Confidence gate** — no switch unless `conf ≥ τ_conf`.

**Exploration uses a staggered scheme.** At each evaluated period, uniform-random arm selection is forced for the SKUs in `{i : hash(i + t) mod 9 == 0}` — about one ninth of SKUs per period. Every SKU therefore receives about `60/9 ≈ 6.7` forced decisions spread across the window, and **no period is systematically degraded for any arm**. **[State why the staggered scheme was chosen: the alternative — forcing random selection for the first 12 periods of the evaluated window — would have made 20 % of CAFR's scored periods deliberately random while the comparator selected normally throughout, handicapping CAFR by design.]**

**The equal-churn comparison is the paper's headline methodology.** To claim "at equal model churn":
1. Run arm (f). Record the mean model churn per SKU, `χ̄_f`.
2. Arm (c) switches when the incumbent's MASE is beaten by more than a margin `m`. Sweep `m` on the **validation** split only.
3. Choose `m*` so that arm (c)'s validation churn equals `χ̄_f` within ±2 %.
4. **Freeze `m*`.** Run arm (c) on the **test** split and report the **realised** churn, not the target.
5. Compare on the test split at the matched churn.

As a second, assumption-free method we keep only the top `k_i` of arm (c)'s candidate switch points ranked by MASE improvement, where `k_i` is arm (f)'s switch count for that SKU. **Both methods are reported; if the conclusion flips between them, that is a finding.**

**[Say explicitly that this is not circular: the churn matching is done on validation and the comparison on test, and the constraint is imposed on the comparator, not on CAFR. A reviewer will look for this.]**

**Two panel facts must be stated as limitations, not hidden.**
1. **RUF has 60 periods, not the 84 the specification assumed.** With a 24-period burn-in, **[36]** periods remain to be evaluated. We reduce `W_occ` accordingly and **report C3's recall separately for the SKUs on which the horizon fits**, alongside the pooled figure. This is a declared config parameter, not a workaround.
2. **RUF is sparsity-limited, not window-limited.** C3's usable fraction barely moves between `W_occ = 36` and `W_occ = 60` (**51.6 % → 60.7 %**). Shortening the window costs little; **the binding constraint is that the median SKU has only 12 nonzero periods in 60 months.** This is a real property of intermittent demand, not a defect of the design, and it should be said in the paper in exactly those terms.

#### 5.3.9 The arms, and the experimental design

All of the following are implemented and reported — **including the arms expected to lose.** Selective reporting of arms is the most likely reviewer objection to an ablation-heavy paper.

| Arm | What it is |
|---|---|
| **(a)** | Fixed best classical method per SKU, chosen once on validation |
| **(b)** | Syntetos–Boylan rule-based selection (ADI × CV² → method), fixed after initialisation |
| **(c)** | **Accuracy-based adaptive selection** — the model with the lowest rolling MASE each period. ★ **The key comparator for H1.** |
| **(d)** | Drift-triggered full model switching (EMC-style dual thresholds with hysteresis) |
| **(e)** | Global LightGBM, pooled across SKUs |
| **(f)** | **CAFR — the full pipeline** |
| **(f′)** | CAFR with the **constrained** bandit: the attributed cause determines the arm set (its matched remedy plus R0) |
| **(g)** | CAFR with a **fixed, hand-written** remedy table — always apply the attributed cause's remedy, no learning |
| **(h)** | CAFR **without** the no-action arm |
| **A4** | ★ **Required comparator.** The same bandit with the same action space R0–R7, the same reward, the same action schedule, the same dwell, the same confidence gate and the same forgetting mechanism — **but with the attribution features removed from the context.** It sees `x^inv` only. |

**A4 is not an optional ablation.** It isolates attribution from everything else and carries the paper's **co-primary hypothesis, H1′**. Its wiring differs from (f) by exactly one thing: the context block.

**Two pairs of arms differ by exactly one thing, and the paper should say so.**
- **(c) vs (f):** (c) selects on MASE and churns freely; (f) selects on cost/service under a churn constraint. Everything else — forecaster pool, simulator, seeds, `fit_on` — is identical.
- **(f) vs A4:** only the context block.
- **(f) vs A5 (an ablation, not an arm):** only the reward content.

**The negative control, run before any positive result is trusted.** Arm (c) versus arm (c), with different random seeds, must show a **null** difference with a 95 % confidence interval that includes zero. If it does not, the harness has a bug.

#### 5.3.10 Statistics

**Sign convention — state it once, use it everywhere.** For any two arms `X` and `Y`, the reported difference is **`cost(X) − cost(Y)`: positive means X is worse.** So `(c) − (f) > 0` means (f) is better, and `(f) − A4 > 0` means (f) is better.

**The primary test is the Wilcoxon signed-rank test on paired per-SKU cost differences** — non-parametric, because cost distributions are heavily skewed. A paired t-test is reported as a cross-check; if the two disagree, trust Wilcoxon and say so. Confidence intervals come from a **bootstrap over SKUs with 10,000 resamples**, and the bootstrap is **repeated clustered by generator parameter cell** as well as by SKU, because on the synthetic panel SKUs sharing a parameter draw are not independent. If the two clusterings disagree, the parameter-cell clustering is the conservative one and is the one to report.

**With 5,000 SKUs, statistical significance is nearly free** — a 0.1 % cost difference will be "significant". Therefore **effect size and the confidence interval are the primary report**, `p`-values are secondary, and we state a **region of practical equivalence of 2 %**: a difference below 2 % is reported as **"no practical difference"** regardless of `p`. We also report the **fraction of SKUs on which (f) beats its comparator** — a distributional, interpretable statistic.

**Multiplicity** is controlled with **Holm–Bonferroni** at a family-wise α = 0.05 across the primary arm-pair family — the (f)-versus-everything comparisons, which include (f)–A4 and (f)–(g). The ablations form a **separate**, explicitly stated family. An undefined family is a p-hacking vector.

**Pre-registration.** The success criteria are fixed before the single test-split run, including the pre-commitment in §5.3.11.

#### 5.3.11 The pre-registered hypotheses

| ID | Role | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| **H1** | **Precondition** (necessary, not sufficient) | At matched model churn, (f) achieves a better inventory cost–service trade-off than (c). Reported as **confirmation of the cited benchmark** (ρ = −0.555), not as the scientific claim. |
| **H1′** | **★ CO-PRIMARY — the scientific claim** | At matched churn, (f) beats **A4** — the same bandit, the same action space, the same reward, the same action schedule, with attribution features removed. |
| **H1″** | Secondary (RQ2) | At matched churn, (f) ≥ (g) — the learned mapping beats the fixed hand-written table. |

**H1 and H1′ are supported** only if, on the test split, all of these hold: the paired mean total-cost difference is positive with a **95 % bootstrap CI excluding zero**; the **effect size is ≥ 2 %** of the comparator's mean cost; the result holds at **≥ 2 of the 3** swept target service levels, and at **no** target is the comparator significantly better after Holm correction; and the result survives the seed sweep (**≥ 4 of 5 seeds** in the same direction) and a **±20 % threshold perturbation**.

**Secondary hypotheses.** **H2** — attribution macro-F1 **≥ 0.70 over the seven real causes C1–C7** and minimum per-class F1 **≥ 0.40**, with a false-positive rate **≤ 10 %** on the no-cause control panel. **H3** — (f) beats (g) with a positive CI; or, if it does not, report the policy–table agreement rate and write it up as *"the attribution alone carries the gain"*. **H4** — descriptive only: report the non-forecast action share (R2, R7, R0) with a bootstrap CI and state whether the CI excludes zero. **H5** — ablation A5 is **worse** than (f) with a CI excluding zero.

**Explicitly NOT required for success — and say this in the paper.** CAFR need not win on **forecast accuracy**; if it does not, that is *expected*, and it must **not** be used as evidence for H1 or H1′. CAFR need not win on every panel. Attribution need not be perfect.

**What would falsify the whole approach.** If attribution macro-F1 over C1–C7 is **below 0.50**, the premise fails: the mechanisms are not separable, and RQ1's answer is no. That is a publishable negative finding about the limits of forecast-failure diagnosis — but it is **a different paper** from the one planned, and the paper should say so plainly rather than report a degraded version of the original claim.

**★ THE PRE-COMMITMENT (§29.4) — put this sentence in the paper verbatim, in the Methods and again in the Discussion:**

> **If H1′ fails while H1 holds, the paper's contribution is the reward signal and the action space, not the attribution — and the paper says so in exactly those words.**

### 5.4 Draft Discussion — limitations paragraph

**You can write this now.** It does not depend on any result, and writing it early protects you from the temptation to soften it later.

> **Limitations.** Four limits bound what this study can claim.
>
> **The primary panel is synthetic, and small.** RUF is a fidelity-certified surrogate of a confidential industrial panel, not the industrial panel itself, and the generators behind it are not released. It carries **5,000 SKUs over 60 monthly periods**. That is a short panel: with a 24-period burn-in, only **[36]** periods remain for evaluation. It is long enough for the one-step-ahead comparison we run, but it is not a long-horizon study. Crucially, **no panel we use carries ground-truth cause labels** — that is why the attribution results are measured on a labelled synthetic panel we generate ourselves, and why the real-panel results test only whether the *gain* transfers, never whether the *attribution* is correct.
>
> **The evaluated window is shorter than the specification assumed, and the panel is sparsity-limited rather than window-limited.** We report C3's recall separately for the SKUs on which the detection horizon fits, alongside the pooled figure. The binding constraint is that the median SKU has only **12 nonzero periods in 60 months**. This is a genuine property of intermittent demand, and it means that some causes are detectable in principle only for the denser half of the panel.
>
> **Censoring is present but unobserved on the real panel, so `fit_on = observed` is forced there.** RUF is a demand panel, not an inventory transaction log: it carries no stock-out field. An unobserved stock-out cannot be imputed, so the forecaster must be fitted on the observed series on that panel. The consequence is that the censoring guard has no flag to read on RUF, and **the guard's value is therefore measured only on the labelled synthetic panel**, where the simulator knows the truth. This is a property of the data, not a design choice. It also means a policy→forecaster feedback loop — policy sets the safety factor → stock-outs → demand censored → the forecaster learns a lower level → the monitor sees bias → attribution fires — is **retained but only partly measurable** on the real panel. It is realistic, and we keep it deliberately, but we cannot fully separate it there.
>
> **Twenty-six per cent of the real panel's SKUs can never fire the intermittency-change cause.** At a 24-period burn-in, **1,304 of 5,000 SKUs (26.1 %)** have fewer than two nonzero periods, so their dispersion statistic is undefined and their initial intermittency cell is labelled `dead`. Those SKUs can never fire C6 — there is no cell to move from — and they can never reach C1's or C3's evidence floors, so they abstain by construction. We keep them, label them, and report the count in **T1**, and we report per-cause recall both pooled and with the `dead` SKUs excluded. Dropping them would be selecting the panel to suit the method.
>
> **The selector is a rule-based abstraction of a learned one, the evaluation is one-step-ahead, and there is no external validation.** The decision layer combines hand-written detection rules with a contextual bandit over a small remedy set; the seven causes are a design choice, not a discovered taxonomy. The inventory comparison is one-step-ahead under a periodic-review order-up-to policy with a fixed lead time, and every result comes from a simulator we built and seeded, not from a live planning system. We run a second, differently-shaped panel as a transfer check, but **we have no external validation on an industrial deployment**, and we make no claim about how these effects scale to a real planner's data.

**Two more things to put in the Discussion, if the results support them.**
- **Where the gain came from.** Attribute each period's cost difference to the action type that was active. The result is a stacked decomposition: how much came from forecast-model remedies (R3–R6) and how much from non-forecast remedies (R2, R7, R0). This is the paper's most quotable result and it is where the "non-forecast actions in the action space" claim earns its place.
- **The honest counterpoint on the patent.** Patent US20100125489 is prior art for "diagnose a cause → adjust forecasts *and* policy parameters → loop". Say so. Then distinguish on four things: not intermittent or spare parts; root-cause language only in dependent claims; human confirmation by consensus; no mechanism taxonomy.

### 6.4 Honest-reporting rules — non-negotiable

1. **Never report a number that came from a notebook.** Everything reported comes from the CLI, with a config and a seed.
2. **Every number in the paper traces to a config file and a seed.** Someone must be able to re-run one command and reproduce it.
3. **Never invent, estimate, or "reconstruct" a result.** A negative result reported honestly is a legitimate paper; a fabricated positive one is misconduct.
4. **Report the nulls.** An ablation table where nothing differs is a finding about the design. Hiding it is the fastest route to a rejection.
5. **Report every gate outcome, including the ones that failed.** If a gate failed, say so and say what was done about it.
6. **Report what was not run.** A clearly-scoped partial result is publishable; a result that pretends to be complete is not.
7. **Never report churn as a mean only.** Report the distribution of inter-switch intervals.
8. **State the sign convention once and apply it without exception.** `cost(X) − cost(Y)`; positive means X is worse.
9. **Never tune anything on the test split.** Tune on validation, freeze every threshold before the single test run, and say so.
10. **Unify on the 2 % region of practical equivalence.** Differences below 2 % are "no practical difference", regardless of `p`.
11. **Do not claim forecast-accuracy wins as evidence for H1 or H1′.** If CAFR does not win on accuracy, that is expected.
12. **★ Honour the §29.4 pre-commitment.** It reads, in full:

> **If H1′ fails while H1 holds, the paper's contribution is the reward signal and the action space, not the attribution — and the paper says so in exactly those words.**

13. **Never write "100% unique", "the first ever", or "nobody has done this".** Never claim Scopus or Web of Science. Use the §4.3 wording verbatim.
