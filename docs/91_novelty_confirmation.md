## 2. NEW NEIGHBOURS FOUND AND VERIFIED THIS ROUND

Each was read (or its abstract read, where full text was paywalled) and checked against the four CAFR criteria:
**(1)** mechanism-level attribution of *why* a forecast failed; **(2)** matched remedy, incl. **non-forecast** actions; **(3)** mapping **learned** from inventory service/cost, not accuracy; **(4)** explicit churn control.

### 2.1 ⚠️ **THE CLOSEST YET — "Predict-then-Correct" (PtC)**
**Lei, Ma & Jackson, arXiv 2607.16354, July 2026** — *A Predict-then-Correct Loop Based on Few-Shot Continuous Contextual Bandit for Demand Forecasting.*

**Why it is the closest yet:** it is a *bandit-driven correction loop* bolted onto a demand forecast, evaluated on M5 and an industrial beverage panel spanning "erratic & intermittent" demand, and it reports **lower inventory costs** than base-stock, PPO and SAC.

**Why it does not take CAFR — verified against the full text, not the abstract:**

| Criterion | PtC | Evidence (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — attribution | **No** | No module explains why the baseline failed. Context carries only a raw error summary ("Historical ML error"). |
| 2 — remedy space | **No** | The action space is **one continuous multiplicative correction factor**: "the action is defined as the continuous adjustment factor applied to the ML-predicted demand", clipped to [−1, 2]. No policy action, no interval action, no discrete remedy set. |
| 3 — learned from service | **No** | The reward is **forecast error**: "the reward is defined as the relative degree of improvement after correction", `r_t = τ(e_ML,t − e_CB,t)`. "maximizing the reward is equivalent to minimizing the corrected prediction error." Inventory cost appears only as a downstream comparison, never as the learning signal. |
| 4 — churn control | **No** | Not addressed. |
| — occurrence/size split | **No** | Errors are magnitude-only. |
| — intermittent coverage | **Weak** | Lumpy SKUs were **deliberately excluded**: "lumpy demand has long zero-demand periods and irregular spikes, making statistical and AI/ML models largely ineffective." |

> **Verdict: a real neighbour, not a killer.** PtC learns *how much* to nudge a forecast on an **accuracy** reward. CAFR asks *which* of several remedies — including ones that never touch the forecast — should be applied, learned from **service/cost**. PtC is, in effect, a degenerate single-action case of CAFR's action space. **Cite it prominently and cite it first**; it is the paper a reviewer will raise.

### 2.2 ⚠️ **A patent that pre-dates much of this**
**US20100125489** — *System and Method for Root Cause Analysis and Early Warning of Inventory Problems.*

Described actions include "adjustments to demand forecasts" **and** "adjustments to published inventory policy parameters (such as safety stock targets)"; the system is described as "self-tuning with each decision-action loop turning into a cycle of learning" and as a "Plan-Do-Check-Act process that facilitates an auto-learning and self-tuning system."

**Limits — and they are real:**
- Root-cause language sits in **dependent claim 7/14 and the description**, not the independent claims (1/8/15 only recite "optimize the inventory policy parameters").
- **Never mentions spare parts or intermittent demand.** Nearest concepts are demand "lumpiness" and a de-causalled signal.
- **Human in the loop:** the planner "confirms root cause via collaboration and consensus", and causes are prioritised "according to business rules."
- No forecast-failure **mechanism taxonomy**; no learned cause→remedy map.

> **Verdict: must be cited and distinguished.** It is prior art for "diagnose a cause → adjust forecasts *and* policy parameters → loop". It is **not** prior art for an automated, mechanism-level, intermittently-demand-specific, service-learned repair layer. (Not legal advice — a content summary.)

### 2.3 **LOWDII** — arXiv 2607.19835, July 2026
"A diagnostic that evaluates the distributional influence of each historical forecast error on the empirical uncertainty distribution", feeding "safety stock calibration"; achieves target service level while cutting average stock 5.1–22.6 %.

**Verdict: not a threat, but the closest thing to CAFR's cause-7 machinery.** It is a *statistical* refinement of the error distribution — no mechanism attributed, no remedy chosen, and the setting is **FMCG, not intermittent or spare parts**. It occupies exactly one corner of CAFR's action space (error diagnosis → adjust safety stock) **without any attribution or selection step**. Cite it when justifying cause 7.

### 2.4 **AHSIV / AHS** — arXiv 2602.13939, 2026
Adaptive automatic model selection for demand forecasting "under Horizon-Induced Degradation": an "automatic model-selection rule that combines observed predictive performance with structural demand information", using RMSSE, MAE, sMAPE, BIAS.

**Verdict: not a threat — it is a strong *comparator*.** No attribution, no non-forecast actions, **no churn control**, and not intermittent (M3/M4/M5, Walmart). This is essentially arm **(c)/(d)** of the planned experiment (`08` §H), so cite it as the state of the art in adaptive selection.

### 2.5 **LCMA** — KBS 2026, DOI `10.1016/j.knosys.2026.116334`
LLM-empowered collaborative multi-agent framework for intermittent demand forecasting of large-scale spare parts. A "Model Selection Agent — uses a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) mechanism to dynamically match forecasting models to demand patterns."

**Verdict: not a threat; the sharpest contrast case.** It classifies **demand *patterns*** (a static property of the series) and its only action is *match a model to a pattern* — objective is "forecasting accuracy and error control". No failure diagnosis, no occurrence/size split, no inventory or policy actions. **It is the clearest example of "the model-switching paradigm CAFR is arguing against", and its whole design is dynamic per-pattern switching — so churn control is not merely absent from this literature, it is contravened by it.** Good foil for the introduction.

### 2.6 **After-sales diagnostic loop** — arXiv 2510.01006, 2025
Loop "closing the loop between forecasting, monitoring, and inventory decisions" over ~6,000 after-sales parts in 90+ countries; "ranked root causes tied to high-impact part-country pairs"; "bias decomposition (over- vs under-forecast)"; regime change-point detection.

**Verdict: partial neighbour.** Diagnostics exist, but they are **bias direction + lifecycle/seasonal drivers + regime shifts** — no mechanism taxonomy; and critically **there is no action set at all**: it asks "which levers should we pull?" without enumerating them. Objective is accuracy (WMAPE), not service/cost. No churn control. Cite as the nearest *industry-facing* diagnostic-loop preprint.

### 2.7 **NNARMA** — EJOR 2026, DOI `10.1016/j.ejor.2026.06.009`
Non-negative ARMA for intermittent demand with "approximate prediction intervals under Normal and Negative Binomial distributions, making the approach suitable for stock control applications"; NNARMA(1,1) "leads to the highest inventory efficiency in most cases."

**Verdict: not a threat.** One *estimator* with intervals feeding stock control. Confirms REJ-9 (interval work for intermittent demand is taken) and reinforces that the **forecast→inventory** link is well studied; only the **failure→cause→remedy** link is not.

### 2.8 **Tensor-optimisation interval prediction** — Sensors 2023, DOI `10.3390/s23167182`
"Adaptive interval prediction algorithm with a dynamic update mechanism", giving "a reliable elastic prediction interval when distortion occurs."

**Verdict: not a threat.** Interval *widening* exists as a *method*; it does not exist as a *diagnosed remedy* selected because mis-calibration was attributed. This is one remedy in CAFR's library, already available off the shelf — which is a **strength** (CAFR need not invent its remedies, only the selection of them).

### 2.9 **Decision-aware Stochastic Consumption Forecasting** — Zenodo 2026, DOI `10.5281/zenodo.18364003`
"Emphasizes model diagnostics, uncertainty interpretation, and decision relevance over point-estimate accuracy"; and, notably, "some operational processes are not well-suited for automated forecasting and are better managed through expert oversight."

**Verdict: not a threat, but the only prior statement of a no-action option found anywhere.** It is a non-peer-reviewed computational notebook and the abstention is a closing remark ("expert oversight"), not a learned action. Cite as the one precedent for the *idea* that abstaining is sometimes right.

### 2.10 **RL inventory control, non-stationary demand** — SSRN 2025, DOI `10.2139/ssrn.5398041`; and **closed-loop prediction-inventory-scheduling** — AMIT 2025, DOI `10.62177/amit.v1i4.507`
The latter combines "LSTM/Transformer demand forecasting, reinforcement learning inventory replenishment, and VRP path planning" with "closed-loop feedback mechanisms".

**Verdict: not threats.** RL over *replenishment* is a different action space from RL over *forecast repair*; neither diagnoses why a forecast failed. Note the AMIT venue is low-quality — cite descriptively only.

### 2.11 **SHOS** — Sci Rep 2026, DOI `10.1038/s41598-026-57129-6`
"Embeds occurrence-size structure directly into supervised learning pipelines"; gains reported purely as MAE −46.3 % / wMAPE > 40 %.

**Verdict: not a threat.** Occurrence/size used as **feature engineering**, not as **diagnosis**. Third independent confirmation that the occurrence/size split is used for *modelling* and never for *attribution* (cf. M30).

### 2.12 **Industry practice** — ToolsGroup, PTC/Servigistics, Blue Yonder, RELEX
ToolsGroup advises diagnosing long-tail demand drivers before changing methods and controls human overreaction by limiting overrides and requiring review for large one-time buys. PTC/Servigistics "Performance Analytics and Intelligence" exposes "systemic or macro-level issues requiring tuning of the planning model itself". Blue Yonder sells "Install Base & Causal Forecasting".

**Verdict: strengthens the case (§A7 in `08`).** The market repeatedly reaches for diagnosis-then-action and ships it as human-in-the-loop product. **No vendor publishes a method**, and none of them defines a failure-mechanism taxonomy or a learned cause→remedy map. This is evidence of demand for CAFR, not pre-emption of it.

### 2.13 **Dataset confirmation (important)**
**`2609.13840` is accepted at ICDM-26** (IEEE International Conference on Data Mining), not merely an arXiv preprint — so reviewers will know it.

**RUF is confirmed as described in `08` §G:** RUF = *Regenerate-Until-Fidelity*, a fidelity-certified synthetic-panel generator; the release includes a **5,000-material surrogate, seed 42, datasheet included, CC-BY-4.0**. It is a surrogate for the confidential industrial panel. Do **not** confuse it with **RAF** (also 5,000 series, 84 monthly periods, ~90 % zeros) — RAF is the *real* public panel and still has **no stated licence**.

> **Dataset plan stands unchanged:** RUF primary (licence-clean), RAF only if its licence is resolved, M5 as a second profile, plus the 2601.21844 generator for the **ground-truth-cause panel** without which attribution accuracy cannot be measured at all.

### 3a. The one IEEE hit worth citing: stock-out distortion *(new neighbour)*

**I Made Debrio Amarta**, *Stock-Out Distortion and Under-Forecasting: An Inventory-Aware Diagnosis of Demand-Signal Bias in Multi-SKU Sales Forecasting*, **ISNCC 2026**, Bristol UK, DOI `10.1109/ISNCC70543.2026.11693859` (added to Xplore 22 Sept 2026 — the day before this search).

**What it does:** diagnoses one specific cause of forecast failure — **stock-out censoring**. "Observed sales are only an imperfect proxy for demand. When an item is out of stock the recorded quantity falls even though customers still want it, and the signal used to train forecasting models is distorted as a result." It reads stock-availability patterns to locate likely-distorted periods, then ties them to forecast behaviour across SARIMA, XGBoost (Tweedie) and CNN-LSTM on 33 lubricant/grease SKUs (PT XYZ, 2021–2025). All models carry negative bias (−2.57, −3.08, −4.80) and under-forecast more often than they over-forecast.

**Verdict: NOT A THREAT — but cite it as a genuine neighbour, and as support for CAFR's premise.** It satisfies only a fragment of criterion (1): **one** cause (data censoring), diagnosed with no remedy selection, no action space, no learning loop, and no service/cost objective. Its own contribution list is "a reading of stock-availability patterns… a diagnosis… a robustness analysis… a computational-cost characterisation" — **a diagnostic study, not a repair system**.

**Distinguish on two specific points:**
1. **It is not an intermittent-demand paper** — and its own finding says the effect *weakens* on intermittent SKUs: "this under-forecasting concentrates in regularly selling SKUs… and weakens on intermittent SKUs whose zeros are largely structural." CAFR's subject is precisely where this paper's mechanism does **not** apply.
2. **Diagnosis is where it stops.** CAFR's claim begins where this ends: having attributed a cause, which remedy — including non-forecast remedies — and learned from service feedback.

Useful framing for the introduction: this paper is the clearest recent evidence that authors are starting to ask *why* forecasts fail, and that the field currently stops at the diagnosis.

**Claim, at its true strength:**
