## 1. FINAL PROJECT

### 1.1 Title (working)

> **Cause-Attributed Forecast Repair for Intermittent Spare-Parts Demand: Learning a
> Mechanism→Remedy Mapping from Inventory Service Feedback**

### 1.2 One-line problem

Standard practice for intermittent spare-parts demand is to pick the forecasting model with
the best accuracy score and re-pick it continuously. That practice has three documented
failures: **accuracy does not predict service** (Spearman ρ = **−0.555** across 38 methods,
arXiv `2609.13840`), **it never asks why** a forecast failed, and **it churns** without
measuring the cost of churning.

### 1.3 Research question (verbatim from Rev 2 §1)

> For intermittent industrial spare-parts demand, does attributing each forecast failure to
> a specific **mechanism** — persistent bias, uncertainty mis-calibration, occurrence
> failure, size failure, level shift, intermittency change, or inventory-policy mis-set —
> and applying the **matched** corrective action, produce a better inventory cost–service
> trade-off than selecting a forecasting model by forecast accuracy, **at the same level of
> model churn**?

**Sub-questions.** RQ1 (attribution): can these mechanisms be separated from *observable*
signals alone? RQ2 (learning): does a **learned** cause→remedy mapping beat a fixed
hand-written table? RQ3 (decomposition): how much of the gain comes from **non-forecast**
remedies?

### 1.4 The gap (Rev 2 §2.1 — the four-part seam)

No work was found that does **all four** of these together:

1. Classify **why an intermittent-demand forecast failed**, by mechanism.
2. Map each mechanism to a **matched remedy, including non-forecast actions** (recalibrate
   the interval, adjust the policy, or do nothing).
3. **Learn** that mapping from **downstream inventory service/cost feedback** — not from
   accuracy.
4. **Explicitly control model/action churn** by design.

### 1.5 Contribution (verbatim from file 08 §E — do not weaken or inflate)

> **We introduce a cause-attributed forecast-repair layer for intermittent spare-parts
> demand that classifies each forecast failure by mechanism — separating occurrence failure,
> size failure, level shift, intermittency change, uncertainty mis-calibration, and
> inventory-policy mis-set — and selects the matched corrective action, where the
> cause→remedy mapping is learned from downstream inventory service-level feedback rather
> than from forecast accuracy.**

**Falsifiable claim (the paper's centrepiece):**

> *At equal model churn, cause-attributed repair beats accuracy-based model selection on the
> inventory cost–service frontier.*

If the experiment does not show this, **the claim fails and the paper says so.**

### 1.6 What IS novel

Exactly three things, and nothing else:

| # | Element | Why it is new |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The action space**: mechanism-matched remedies **including non-forecast actions** (R2 conformal recalibration, R7 service-probability controller, R0 do-nothing) inside one bandit | PtC (arXiv `2607.16354`) has **one** continuous multiplicative factor. Everything else in the field acts on the forecast only. |
| 2 | **The reward signal**: realised inventory cost/service, never forecast error | PtC's reward is `r_t = τ(e_ML,t − e_CB,t)` — forecast error. |
| 3 | **The attribution→selection link**, measured against an ablation that removes it | A4 (§2.6) is the arm that isolates it. This is hypothesis **H1′**, the co-primary claim. |

### 1.7 What is **NOT** claimed novel — say these plainly in the paper

- **The bandit is not novel.** arXiv `2310.16096` and PtC `2607.16354` both already put
  bandits on inventory/demand. Rev 2 §4 says to say so openly.
- **The remedies are not novel.** Interval widening, bias correction, safety-stock
  adjustment and TSB all exist off the shelf. **CAFR need not invent the remedies — only
  the selection of them.** Rev 2 §7.2: this is a strength, not a weakness; it moves the
  novelty claim to where it actually lives.
- **The forecast-error taxonomy concept is not ours.** Clements & Hendry
  (`10.1007/978-1-4614-1653-1_9`) established a general forecast-error taxonomy with
  cause-matched remedies (intercept corrections). CAFR **operationalises** that idea for
  intermittent demand + inventory. Frame it explicitly as operationalisation. **Never claim
  the taxonomy concept.**
- **The no-action option has one prior statement**: a closing remark in a non-peer-reviewed
  notebook, Zenodo `10.5281/zenodo.18364003`. Cite it as the one precedent for the *idea*.
  Do not claim it as ours.

### 1.8 Required novelty wording (Rev 2 §2.2 — do not deviate)

> **No directly matching work was found in the searched databases (OpenAlex, IEEE Xplore,
> Crossref, web search). Scopus and Web of Science were not searched.**

Never write "100% unique", "the first ever", or "nobody has done this". **Scopus and Web of
Science were never searched** (no access) — never claim them.

### 1.9 Industrial relevance

Spare-parts planning is periodic-review, order-up-to, and dominated by intermittent demand:
~70 % of SKU-months have zero demand (RUF's actual figure: **0.7008**). Service levels are
contractual. The paper's own cited benchmark shows that choosing the most accurate model can
make service **worse** (ρ = −0.555). A planner needs to know *why* a forecast failed before
changing anything — and sometimes the answer is "the forecast is fine, your safety stock is
wrong", which no accuracy-based selection can ever discover.

## 2. EXACT METHODOLOGY — PLAIN IMPLEMENTATION LANGUAGE

### 2.1 The loop, in one picture

```
  demand data (observed, possibly censored)
        │
        ▼
  M0  FORECASTER POOL ── Croston, SBA, TSB, mSBA, mTSB, SES-on-sizes, global LightGBM
        │                 (point + quantiles; fitted on `fit_on`)
        ▼
  M1  OUTCOME MONITOR ── residuals, bias, coverage, ADI, CV², TWO CUSUMs, CSL/PIS/NOS
        │                 preceded by the C-GUARD
        ▼
  M2  ATTRIBUTION ────── one of C1..C7, or C0 (abstain), plus a confidence score
        │                 RULES FIRST, classifier optional
        ▼
  M3  REMEDY LIBRARY ─── R0..R7, one matched action per cause
        │
        ▼
  M4  SELECTION POLICY ─ Thompson-sampling contextual bandit, reward = cost/service,
        │                 dwell-time + confidence-gate + forgetting
        ▼
  M5  INVENTORY SIM ──── periodic review (S,S), lead time L, censoring, cost, CSL
        │
        └──────────────► the realised cost/service becomes the reward → M4 updates
```

**One decision point, timed exactly (Rev 2 §10).** At the end of review period *t* for SKU
*i*: M1 emits the feature vector → M2 emits (cause, confidence) → M4 selects a remedy from
M3's library (or abstains) → the action is applied → M5 advances the simulator one period
with the action in force → the realised cost/service becomes the reward → the bandit
updates. **One period of delay is mandatory**: the reward must be the outcome of the action,
never of a period the action did not affect.

### 2.2 The seven causes + the abstain (Rev 2 §5)

A cause is a **hypothesis about the mechanism** that produced the observed error. It is not a
description of the error. Two causes can produce similar errors — that overlap is the
substance of RQ1, not a defect.

| ID | Cause | One-line definition |
|---|---|---|
| **C1** | Persistent directional bias | The forecaster is systematically wrong in one direction, **stable and gradual**. In the **size** sub-process. |
| **C2** | Uncertainty mis-calibration | The point forecast is acceptable but the **predictive interval** is wrong: empirical coverage of the nominal α-interval is persistently off α. |
| **C3** | Occurrence failure | The forecaster misjudges **whether** demand occurs — occurrence AUC is low, or the false-zero rate is high — while conditional sizes are fine. |
| **C4** | Size failure | Occurrence is predicted acceptably, but the **nonzero size** is mis-modelled: error **dispersion** on nonzero periods is high and growing relative to the SKU's own baseline, **at an unchanged mean**. |
| **C5** | Level / regime shift | An **abrupt** change in the DGP — a step in level or in occurrence rate — the estimator has not absorbed because it still averages over pre-shift data. |
| **C6** | Intermittency change | The SKU's **intermittency cell** has crossed a boundary and stayed there, so the method family in use is no longer appropriate. Boundary is the reachable one (§2.4), **not** the textbook ADI 1.32. |
| **C7** | Inventory-policy mis-set | The forecast is **acceptable** by every other test, yet achieved service persistently misses target. The fault is in the policy parameters, not the forecast. |
| **C0** | No actionable signal *(not a cause — the abstain)* | Confidence below threshold. **Do nothing.** A first-class action, not a fallback. |

> **Two definitional amendments in Rev 2 — do not treat them as errors.**
> (1) **C1 no longer requires bias in *both* sub-processes.** Under the corrected C1
> injection the ramp is in `μ_z` only, so occurrence bias ≈ 0 — the old gate would have made
> C1 undetectable. Occurrence bias is still computed and **reported**, but it is not a gate.
> (2) **C6's boundary is `ADI* = 4.0`, not 1.32.** A spare-parts panel has ADI ≥ 2 by
> construction (`ADI = 1/p`, `p ≤ 0.50`), so 1.32 can never be crossed. **The name and
> meaning of C6 are unchanged; only the numerical boundary moves, from one that cannot be
> crossed to one that can.**

### 2.3 The remedies (Rev 2 §7)

| Cause | Remedy | Exact action |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **R1** Damped intercept correction | Add `λ·b` to the point forecast, where `b` is the recent mean residual (occurrence and size corrected **separately**). **Default λ = 1.0 for occurrence, λ = 0.5 for size** (size residuals are noisier). Continuous — not a model switch. |
| C2 | **R2** Conformal recalibration | Recompute the empirical quantiles of `A^{agg} − R·μ̂` over the last `W_cal` periods, rebuild `Q̂_α`. Widens or narrows the interval. **Non-forecast: the point forecast is untouched.** |
| C3 | **R3** Switch the occurrence sub-model | TSB (smooths the occurrence probability directly and can decay to zero) or a hurdle classifier. **Size model untouched.** |
| C4 | **R4** Re-estimate the size model | Refit on nonzero sizes only, with recency weighting (damped trend, or the pooled LightGBM restricted to nonzero sizes). **Occurrence model untouched.** |
| C5 | **R5** Discard pre-shift data + step indicator | Set the estimation window to `[τ, t]` (the CUSUM change point), re-initialise there, add a step term so the estimator does not smooth across the break. |
| C6 | **R6** Re-classify and re-initialise | Recompute the (ADI, CV²) cell; re-map to a method family through a **fixed, documented mapping table**. (Arm (g) uses the same map.) |
| C7 | **R7** Adjust the safety factor — **leave the forecast alone** | The service-probability controller, §2.5. |
| C0 | **R0** Do nothing | Keep the current forecaster and policy unchanged. |

> **Implementer note on R7 (Rev 2 §7.1).** R7 is what makes C7 more than a label. If R7 is
> implemented as "change the forecast", the paper's central distinction collapses. **It must
> not touch `μ̂`.**

| Bias sign stability (C1) | 4 × `W_short` = **96** | — |

| ADI / CV² cell (C6) | `W_class = 30` | ≥ 30 periods |
| CSL gap (C7) | `W_svc = 30` | ≥ 30 periods **and** ≥ 2 unmet cycles |

> **⚠️ `W_occ` is the binding horizon on the real panels and it does not fit on RUF.** See

### 2.5 The C7 service-level controller (Rev 2 §7.1 — exact)

```
State:   α_t ∈ [ α_min , α_max ]
         α_min = 0.50
         α_max = min( 0.999 , 1 − 1/(n_t + 1) )    n_t = nonzero cycles available for the quantile

Update:  α_{t+1} = clip( α_t + κ · ( α* − ĉ_t ) , α_min , α_max )        κ = 1.0 default

Order-up-to:   S_{i,t} = Q̂_{α_t}(i, t)
```

The state is a **service probability**, not a Gaussian z. `κ`'s units are
*service-probability per unit CSL gap*. The old `z_max = Φ⁻¹(0.999)` is replaced by the
**statistical** limit of what the sample supports — with `n_t = 30` cycles the largest
estimable quantile is ≈ 0.968, so the controller can never demand a quantile the data cannot
estimate. `α_t` is readable by a practitioner ("the policy is currently targeting the 94th
percentile"). Sweep `κ` in E6.

| **(c)** | **Accuracy-based adaptive selection** — pick the model with the lowest rolling MASE each period | ★ **the key comparator for H1** |

### 2.7 Fixed mapping `g` (arm (g)) and the bandit `f` (arm (f))

- **`g` — the fixed table.** Deterministic: attributed cause → its matched remedy, applied
  always. No learning. This is file 08 §F's table, and it is **the safe stopping point**
  (Step 8).
- **`f` — the bandit.** Thompson sampling with a **linear contextual model** (Gaussian
  likelihood, or logistic if the reward is reshaped to a bounded utility). **One global
  policy across all SKUs**, generalising through the context vector. Per-SKU policies are
  not viable and are not attempted.

**Context vector `x_{i,t}`** (all from M1, all observable at decision time). Written
`x_{i,t} = [ x^inv_{i,t} , x^det_{i,t} ]` — this split is what defines A4 exactly.

| Block | Features |
|---|---|
| Attribution (`x^det`) | attributed cause (one-hot), `conf` |
| Error | rolling bias (occ + size), `σ_sz`, size-dispersion ratio `r`, occurrence AUC, false-zero rate, interval coverage |
| Intermittency | ADI, CV², cell (one-hot), mean nonzero size, fraction of zeros |
| Inventory (`x^inv`) | achieved CSL gap `g`, fill rate, avg on-hand, avg backorders, PIS, NOS |
| Control | periods since last switch, current active remedy (one-hot) |
| SKU statics | lead time, unit cost, holding rate, demand class |

**Standardise all continuous features using training-split statistics only.** Report the
feature list and the standardisation constants in the paper (T7).

> **⚠️ Interpretation point that needs the team's confirmation.** Rev 2 §9.2 defines A4's
> context as `x^inv` = "inventory and demand-summary". §9.1's feature list has a **separate
> "Error" block** (bias, coverage, AUC, size dispersion) that §9.2 does not explicitly
> assign. The safe reading — and the one adopted here — is: **A4 receives everything except
> the explicit attribution output** (`x^det` = cause one-hot, confidence, severity). That
> makes A4 as strong as possible, so an (f) > A4 result cannot be dismissed as an unfair
> comparison. **Implement it that way and state the choice in T7.** If the team prefers the
> stricter reading (`x^inv` = inventory only), it is a config change to `context.py` — but
> decide **before** the test run, and record it.

### 2.8 The reward (Rev 2 §9.3, OD-3 frozen)

```
r_{i,t} = − [ ( H · Ī_{i,t+1} + B · B̄_{i,t+1} ) − C̄_i ] / σ_{C,i}

with the σ-floor:   σ_{C,i}  ←  max( σ_{C,i} , 0.05 · C̄_i )
```

`C̄_i`, `σ_{C,i}` are the SKU's burn-in mean and sd of period cost.

**Why the standardisation is mandatory.** Raw cost scales with SKU volume by orders of
magnitude. An unstandardised reward makes the bandit a policy for the three largest SKUs and
noise for the rest.

**Why the σ-floor.** Without it the reward explodes for any SKU with near-constant cost,
and those updates dominate the bandit.

> **⛔ `C̄_i` and `σ_{C,i}` must be recomputed on the corrected simulator (§15.6) BEFORE any
> arm is scored.** This is §30 gate 3.6. Scoring an arm first contaminates the reward scale.

**What the reward is not.** Not forecast error, not MASE, not RMSSE. This is the single
clearest line between CAFR and PtC.

### 2.9 Churn: measurement and control (Rev 2 §11)

```
config_{i,t} = ( base_forecaster_family_{i,t} , active_remedy_{i,t} )
```

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Model churn** `χ^model_i` | fraction of periods where `base_forecaster_family` changed — **the strict one**. R1, R2, R7 do **not** cause model churn (they are parameter adjustments). R3, R4, R5, R6 do. |
| **Action churn** `χ^action_i` | fraction of periods where `active_remedy` changed — includes parameter adjustments. |

**The headline comparison uses `χ^model`**, because that is what "model selection" means in
arm (c) and it is the metric the equal-churn constraint is imposed on. Report both.

> **⛔ Forced-exploration decisions are excluded from every churn count.** A decision the
> policy did not choose is not a switch. Count only decisions where `exploratory == False`.

**Three control mechanisms, in order of strength:**
1. **R0 abstain** — low-confidence cases consume no switch. This is the *design* mechanism.
2. **Dwell time `d`** — after any action at `t`, no further switch until `t + d`. **Default
   d = 3.** Exception: a **C5 CUSUM alarm** (either sub-process) overrides the dwell. **Log
   every override.**
3. **Confidence gate** — no switch unless `conf ≥ τ_conf`.

**Equal-churn comparison (the headline methodology, Rev 2 §11.3).** To claim "at equal model
churn": run (f), record mean `χ̄_f`; sweep arm (c)'s switch margin `m` on the **validation**
split; pick `m*` so (c)'s validation churn equals `χ̄_f` within ±2 %; **freeze `m*`**; run
(c) on test and confirm the realised churn; report the **realised** value, not the target.
Second, assumption-free method: keep only the top `k_i` of (c)'s candidate switch points,
ranked by MASE improvement, where `k_i` = (f)'s switch count. **Report both**; if the
conclusion flips, that is a finding. The machinery now applies to **three** comparisons:
**(f)–(c)** (H1), **(f)–A4** (H1′), **(f)–(g)** (H1″).

### 2.10 Forced exploration (Rev 2 §9.4) — use the staggered scheme

```
At each evaluated period t, force uniform-random arm selection for the SKUs in
    { i : hash(i + t) mod 9 == 0 }        ≈ 1/9 of SKUs per period
```

Every SKU receives ≈ 60/9 ≈ 6.7 forced decisions spread across the window, and **no period
is systematically degraded for any arm.** With 5,000 SKUs that is ≈ 33,000 forced decisions
— far more than the posterior needs.

> **Do not use Rev 1's scheme.** Rev 1 forced uniform-random selection for the first 12
> periods of the evaluated window — which, with 60 evaluated periods, meant **20 % of (f)'s
> scored periods were deliberately random while (c) selected normally throughout.** That
> handicaps (f) by design. The alternative (a 12-period warm-up excluded from scoring for
> **all** arms) is acceptable but costs 12 periods of window — on RUF you do not have them.

### 2.11 Censoring: the C-guard and `fit_on` (Rev 2 §6.2, §14.3 rule 6)

**The C-guard.** Flag period *t* as potentially censored if the inventory position reached
zero during *t*. `U_i(t) = {s ≤ t : not censored(s)}`. All bias, CUSUM and size-error
statistics for C1/C4/C5 are computed over `U_i(t)` only.

| The guard covers | The guard does **not** cover |
|---|---|
| The bias statistics (C1) | The **forecaster's training input** — handled separately by `fit_on` |
| The size CUSUM (C5) | **Structural zeros** — a genuine zero is not censored and must not be discarded |
| The size-error statistics (C4) | The **achieved CSL and the reward** — there, censored periods *are* the signal |

**`fit_on` — the forecaster's fitting input. An explicit config switch, fixed identically
across all arms and all panels:**

```
forecast.fit_on  ∈  { observed , uncensored , true }        DEFAULT: uncensored

observed     fit on demand_observed. Realistic. Destroys C7's acceptability gate
             → C7 undetectable by construction.
uncensored   DEFAULT. Impute lost sales on stock-out periods, then fit. The modern
             inventory-forecasting practice; the realistic middle path.
true         fit on demand_true. An idealisation — declare it as such in the paper.
```

**The imputation must use observables only, or `uncensored` becomes a leak.** Impute each
stock-out period's lost demand by sampling from that SKU's **own historical nonzero-size
empirical distribution** (estimated on pre-censoring periods), scaled to the number of
periods the SKU was exposed and unmet. **The imputation function may not receive
`demand_lost` or `demand_true`.** Enforce in the unit test.

**Why this matters.** Without it there is a policy→forecaster feedback loop: *policy sets
safety factor → stock-outs → demand censored → forecaster learns a lower level → forecast
degrades → monitor sees bias → attribution fires (possibly the wrong cause) → policy
changes → …* The loop is **realistic** (it is the ISNCC 2026 mechanism) and is **deliberately
retained** — but it must be made explicit and measured. Ablation **A6b** isolates it.

### 2.12 Inventory simulator (M5) — Rev 2 §15.6

- **Periodic review, order-up-to `(S, S)`.** Lead time **`L = 2`** (default; swept in E6).
- **Protection interval `R = L + 1`.** Order placed at end of `t`, arrives start of `t+L`.
- **Order-up-to level — the corrected construction:**

  ```
  S_{i,t}  =  Q̂_α(i,t)
             =  R·μ̂_{i,t}  +  F̂⁻¹(α)          equivalently, empirically:
  where F̂ is the empirical CDF of ( A^{agg}_{i,t} − R·μ̂_{i,t} ) over the trailing window
  and A^{agg} is the R-period AGGREGATE demand.
  Under the C7 controller:  S_{i,t} = Q̂_{α_t}(i,t)
  ```

  **Why this replaces `S = R·μ̂ + z·σ̂`.** Rev 1 estimated `σ̂` from **per-period** errors
  but applied it to the **R-period** protection interval. Under independence the
  protection-interval sd is `σ_R ≈ √R · σ_period`, so for `R = 3` the safety-stock term was
  understated by **42.3 %**. Every arm would have missed its CSL target for a reason with
  nothing to do with any hypothesis. **Do not re-derive this. Use it as written.**

  **The Gaussian `z` is dropped even when `σ̂` is correct** — intermittent errors are
  right-skewed. Use the empirical quantile.
- **Quantile floor:** `α_max = min(0.999, 1 − 1/(n+1))` where `n` = nonzero cycles available.
- **Costs:** `H` per unit-period held, `B` per unit-period backordered. **Sweep
  `B/H ∈ {4, 9, 19, 49}`** (≈ 80/90/95/98 % CSL under a newsvendor reading).
- **Log both censored and true demand**, plus a `stockout_flag`. **The policy sees only the

### 3.4 ⚠️ H-1 — THE PANEL-LENGTH DECISION (the one thing you must not guess)

**The problem, exactly.**

- Rev 2 §14.1 assumes **84 periods**: burn-in 24 + evaluated 60, and says *"RUF has 84, so
  24 + 60 = 84 exactly."*
- **RUF has 60 periods total.** Not 60 evaluated plus 24 burn-in.
- Rev 2 §14.1's stated fallback — *"otherwise reduce `W_occ` to 48"* — **also does not fit**:
  `24 + 48 = 72 > 60`.

So **both rungs of §14.1's resolution ladder fail.** This is the contingency the brief
anticipated. It is flagged, not patched.

**What this actually costs, and what it does not.** It affects **only the real-panel
transfer evaluation**. The labelled synthetic panel **is ours**, is `T = 200` (§15.1), is
unaffected, and still carries the headline comparisons E1 and the attribution gates. E4 must
be run on both panels (Handoff G), so on RUF it runs on the shorter evaluated window.

**The verified arithmetic (measured on the real panel, not estimated):**

|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | 48 | **37.2 %** | C1 48.6 % · C4 38.6 % · C6 62.8 % |
| 18 | 42 | 29.4 % | — |
| **24** | **36** | **26.1 %** | **C1 44.2 % · C4 35.2 % · C6 73.9 %** |
| 30 | 30 | 23.6 % | — |
| 36 | 24 | 22.1 % | — |

**`W_occ` feasibility on a 60-period panel** (verified): `W = 60` yields **0** usable
full-window decision points at any burn-in; `W = 48` → ~12–13 points; `W = 36` → ~24–25
points; `W = 30` → ~30 points.

|---|---|---|---|

| C1 (`W_short = 24`) | — | — | **53.7 %** |
| C4 (`W_sz = 24`) | — | — | **42.3 %** |

C2, C6 and C7 are **not** sparsity-limited (they count all periods, not nonzero ones) and are
correctly absent from this table.

> **The key insight: RUF is sparsity-limited, not window-limited.** C3's usability barely
> moves between `W_occ = 36` and `W_occ = 60` (51.6 % → 60.7 %). Shortening the window
> costs little; the binding constraint is that the median SKU has only 12 nonzero periods
> in 60 months. **Say this in the paper** — it is a real property of intermittent demand,
> not a defect of our design, and gate 3.5's option 3 exists precisely to report it.

**The recommended resolution — H-1 (needs the team's sign-off):**

```
burn-in        = 24 periods     (UNCHANGED from Rev 2)
evaluated      = 36 periods     (was 60)
W_occ          = 36             (was 60; a declared config parameter)
all other 7 monitor horizons unchanged
L = 2, R = 3, fit_on = observed on RUF
```

**Why 24 and not 12.** Burn-in is what initialises the methods, estimates `C̄_i` and
`σ_{C,i}`, computes `adi_init` / `cv2_init` / `sb_cell_init`, and seeds the bandit. At
burn-in 12 the median SKU has ≈ 2.4 nonzero observations — too thin for a size model or for
a period-cost sd (which is why OD-3's σ-floor exists at all). Burn-in 12 also **doubles** the
undefined-cell rate (37.2 % vs 26.1 %). Burn-in 24 is already the value Rev 2 uses to define
the entire `sku_meta` feature layer (§27); changing it changes the feature layer for every
SKU.

**Why reducing `W_occ` is legitimate and reducing it twice is not a violation.** §14.1 rung 2
already contemplates reducing `W_occ` to 48 and calls it "a one-line config change". §6.1
declares all eight horizons config parameters. Going to 36 is the same operation applied
once more, forced by the panel being 24 periods shorter than assumed.

**Two things this decision obliges you to do:**
1. **Gate 3.5 option 3 — always report C3's recall separately for the SKUs where the horizon
   fits, alongside the pooled figure.** This is not optional. It is also §29.3's requirement.
2. **State the evaluated window (36, not 60) in the methods section and in T1.**

**The alternatives, if the team prefers them:**
- **H-1-alt-A: burn-in 12, evaluated 48, `W_occ = 48`.** Preserves more evaluated periods
  (48 vs 36) but doubles the undefined-cell rate and leaves ~2.4 nonzero periods to
  initialise each method. **Choose this only if statistical power in E4 turns out to bind.**
- **H-1-alt-B: get an 84-period panel.** **RAF** (5,000 SKUs × 84 months) is exactly the
  right shape — but it has **no stated licence**, so it is blocked on OD-6 until
  Dr. V. K. Chawla confirms one in writing. **Do not download or use RAF before that.**

tuning a detector.** Rev 2 is explicit that a failing gate is a specification problem, not a

### 3.5 ⚠️ H-2 — an ambiguity in Rev 2 §6.1 that must be settled before you code C3

§6.1 gives C3 the trailing horizon `W_occ` with "Minimum evidence ≥ 10 nonzero periods".
Two readings:

- **Reading A — the window must be FULL.** A `W_occ`-period window counts only when
  `W_occ` periods have actually elapsed. On RUF with `W_occ = 60` this gives **exactly one**
  usable decision point per SKU. Unusable.
- **Reading B — use what is available, floor on the nonzero count.** The window is
  `min(W_occ, available history)`, and the detector abstains until ≥ 10 nonzero periods have
  accumulated. This is standard practice, it is what the "Minimum evidence" column is
  *for*, and it is what makes C3 usable at all.

**Recommended: Reading B** — and note honestly that under Reading B, on a 60-period panel,
`W_occ = 60` degenerates to "the whole observed history", which is a **different statistic**
from a 60-period rolling window on an 84-period panel. That is a further reason to set
`W_occ = 36` (H-1): at 36, Reading B's window is genuinely rolling on RUF rather than
degenerating to the full history.

**Implement Reading B.** Log, per decision point, whether the window was full or partial
(`actions.parquet`). Report it. **Flag it to the team** — it is an interpretation of the
spec, not a change to it.

## 7. IMPLEMENTATION SPECIFICATION

**Do NOT write the whole project code yet.** This section is a specification, not a script.
The only code quoted is where a snippet removes genuine ambiguity.

### 7.1 The inventory simulator (M5) — build this first

```
Periodic review, order-up-to (S, S).
State per SKU: on_hand, backorders (or lost sales), pipeline of orders by arrival period.

Each period t:
  1. Receive arrivals scheduled for t.
  2. Observe demand y_t (drawn from the DGP; the simulator knows the truth).
  3. Fulfil from on_hand. Unmet demand → backorder or lost, per config.
       demand_met   = min(y_t, on_hand)
       demand_lost  = y_t − demand_met            (backorder model: goes to backorders)
       stockout_flag = (on_hand reached zero during t)
  4. Censoring: the POLICY observes  demand_observed = demand_met  (lost-sales model)
                or                 demand_observed = demand_met    (backorder model, if
                unmet backorders are honoured later — state which model you use)
  5. Compute cost:  H · on_hand_end + B · backorders_end
  6. POLICY DECISION (reads only s ≤ t; see §5.6).
  7. Place order:  order_qty = max(0, S_{i,t} − inventory_position)
                   inventory_position = on_hand + on_order − backorders
                   arrives at start of t + L.
```

> **⚠️ Step 7 is where the 42 % bug lives.** `S_{i,t} = Q̂_α(i,t)` — the **R-period
> aggregate** quantile — not `R·μ̂ + z·σ̂_period`. See §2.12. Rev 2 §15.6 derives why:
> applying a per-period sd to an R-period protection interval understates safety stock by
> **42.3 %** at `R = 3`, and the resulting service shortfall has **nothing to do with any
> hypothesis**. Every downstream number inherits this. **This bug is silent** — it produces
> plausible-looking results at the wrong service level.

**`fit_on`.** Before fitting any forecaster, the simulator exposes the censoring structure
needed by §14.3 rule 6. The imputation function's signature must **not** accept `demand_lost`
or `demand_true`. Unit-test it.

**Determinism.** Every stochastic element seeded from `(panel_seed, sku_id, period)`. Not a
running RNG stream — a **derived** seed, so that forking the simulator for the oracle (§7.8)
gives identical future draws across arms.

### 7.2 The forecaster pool (M0)

| Method | Interface | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Croston | `fit(y, fit_on) -> μ̂, ẑ, σ̂` ; `predict(h) -> point, quantiles` | Write it yourself — you must modify it for R3/R4 |
| SBA | same | Croston with the 0.95 bias correction |
| TSB | same | Smooths the occurrence probability directly; **can decay to zero** |
| mSBA, mTSB | same | The "m" variants |
| SES on nonzero sizes | same | Occurrence via a separate Bernoulli rate |
| Global LightGBM | same | **Pooled across SKUs**, with SKU static features + lagged demand. **No SKU-specific training — that would leak.** |

**Validate each against `statsforecast`** on a fixed 200-period series, fixed seed.
**Relative error ≤ 1e-6.** *A silently wrong Croston implementation invalidates every
downstream result and is very easy to ship unnoticed.*

**Requirements:** point forecasts non-negative; quantiles monotone in α; TSB's occurrence
probability decays toward zero on an all-zero series (final `ẑ < 0.01` after 200 zero
periods); LightGBM deterministic (fixed seed, `n_jobs=1`, two runs byte-identical); **every
model respects `fit_on`**, asserted in a unit test.

**Quantiles for the order-up-to level.** The empirical α-quantile of the **R-period
aggregate**, `A^{agg}`, over the trailing window — floor at
`α_max = min(0.999, 1 − 1/(n+1))`.

### 7.3 Forecast intervals

Rev 2 §17. Report **coverage and width together** — coverage without width is meaningless
(a 0–∞ interval always covers).

- **Primary accuracy metric: MASEII** — accounts for occurrence misclassification. Standard
  MASE explodes on intermittent series because the naive-forecast denominator can approach
  zero.
- MASE is secondary, **with the denominator caveat stated.**
- RMSSE is secondary; it is used by C7's acceptability gate and by arm (c)'s selection
  margin. **It is no longer C4's statistic.**
- Interval coverage is measured against the **reported** interval `Q̃_α`, **not** the fitted
  one. If the reported interval is the mis-calibrated object, measuring coverage against the
  fitted one leaves C2 undetectable. **This is the Revision 2 correction.**

> **⛔ OD-8 is open and blocks Step 6+, not Steps 1–5.** File 08 §A3 lists MASE, GMAE,
> MASEII, SPEC, AUC-ROC, PIS, NOS, BDD without pinning formulas. **Adopt the definitions used
> in arXiv `2609.13840`** wherever it defines them, so our numbers compare directly to the
> benchmark our related-work section leans on. Where the benchmark is silent, use the
> original Syntetos–Boylan / Kourentzos definitions and cite them. **Record the source of
> every formula in a code comment.** **Do not silently use `sklearn.metrics` defaults for any
> scaled error — the defaults are wrong for intermittent demand.** (The macro-F1 denominator
> is no longer part of OD-8 — §19 fixes it.)

### 7.4 CUSUM monitoring (M1, C5) — TWO CUSUMs, not one

```
Occurrence CUSUM — detects a step in the occurrence rate p
    e^occ_t  = δ_t − ẑ_t
    σ_occ    = sqrt( mean_t( ẑ_t · (1 − ẑ_t) ) )        over the window
    S⁺_t = max(0, S⁺_{t−1} + e^occ_t − k·σ_occ)
    S⁻_t = max(0, S⁻_{t−1} − e^occ_t − k·σ_occ)
    alarm if max(S⁺, S⁻) > h·σ_occ

Size CUSUM — detects a step in μ_z.  RESTRICTED TO NONZERO PERIODS N_i.
    e^sz_t  = z_t − μ̂^sz_t                              for t ∈ U_i(t) ∩ N_i
    σ_sz    = robust sd of e^sz over the trailing window of nonzero periods
    same recursion with k, h

k = 0.25 ,  h = 4.0   (both sub-processes)
```

> **⛔ Why not one pooled CUSUM (Rev 1's design).** On an intermittent series roughly nine of
> every ten residuals are a moderate **negative constant** (a zero period contributes
> `0 − μ̂ ≈ −p·μ_z`), so the lower arm accumulated steadily through any run of zeros
> **whether or not a shift occurred**. The false-alarm rate was governed by `p` — the lower
> the intermittency, the more false C5 alarms — and **C5 sits FIRST in the priority order**,
> so it pre-empted C1/C4/C6. The correction removes the mechanism rather than compensating
> with a larger threshold. **Do not merge them back.**

**Change point.** On an alarm, set `τ` to the last index at which that statistic was zero
before the alarm. R5 uses `τ` to decide which data to discard.

**False-alarm control.** Measure the in-control ARL **empirically on the C0 control panel**.
**No theoretical ARL value is asserted.**

> **⛔ `k` and `h` are NOT freely tunable.** They were co-designed with the injection
> magnitudes via the detectability budget `Δ ≥ k + h/(p_eff·W_detect)`. Tuning them **after**
> generation silently breaks the budget. If they must change, the panel is **regenerated**
> and gates 4.6 and 4.12 re-checked (§14.2).

### 7.5 The C-guard (M1, mandatory pre-step)

```
flag period t as potentially censored if the inventory position reached zero during t
U_i(t) = { s ≤ t : not censored(s) }
```

All bias, CUSUM and size-error statistics for C1/C4/C5 are computed over `U_i(t)` only. The
complement is **not** dropped from the CSL computation — there it is the signal.

> **Structural zeros must NOT be discarded.** A genuine zero-demand period is not censored.
> This is why the size CUSUM is restricted to `N_i` and the occurrence CUSUM runs on `δ`
> separately.

### 7.6 Attribution — the priority-ordered detector (M2)

```
C-guard  →  C5 (abrupt shift)  →  C1 (gradual bias)
         →  C4 (size dispersion)   →  C2 (calibration, mean OK)
         →  C3 (occurrence)        →  C6 (cell change)
         →  C7 (policy, all else clear)   →  C0
```

**The one change from Rev 1: `C4` now precedes `C2`.** Forced, not arbitrary — a change in
the size distribution necessarily moves coverage, so C4's more specific signature
(dispersion at fixed mean) must be tested before C2's (coverage). Rev 1's order let C2
shadow C4 on **every** C4 series.

**The full condition set for every cause is Rev 2 §6.3. Implement it verbatim.** The key
points a careless implementation gets wrong:

- **C1**: bias is over `W_short = 24`, restricted to `U_i(t)` **AND** nonzero periods. Fire
  when `|z_b| > 1.96` **and** the sign is stable across the last `k = 4` consecutive windows
  **and** the **drift model beats the step model on BIC** (this is the C1/C5 discriminator)
  **and** `n ≥ 8`. **The "both sub-processes" gate is REMOVED** — it would make C1
  undetectable. `b^occ` is computed and reported, not gated.
- **C2**: over `W_cal = 30`, measuring `ĉ` against the **REPORTED** interval `Q̃_α`. Fire when
  `|ĉ − α| > τ_cov` **and** the binomial test rejects at p < 0.05 **and** BOTH guards hold:
  `|z_b| ≤ 1.96` (the mean is acceptable) and `r ≤ 1.5` (C4 is at control).
- **C3**: over `W_occ`, **requires ≥ 10 nonzero periods in the window** else abstain. Fire
  when (`AUC < 0.65` **or** FZ > 0.30) **and** the size-dispersion statistic is not elevated.
- **C4**: over `W_sz = 24`, restricted to `U_i(t) ∩ N_i` where `δ = 1` **and** `ẑ ≥ 0.5`.
  **Use `sd(e^sz)` (MAD-based robust sd), NOT RMSSE** — RMSSE contains the mean term and
  would fire on a C1 or C5 series. Fire when `r > 1.5` **sustained 2 consecutive windows**
  **and** `AUC ≥ 0.65`.
- **C5**: either CUSUM alarms **within the last review period**, **and** the step model beats
  the drift model on BIC.
- **C6**: over `W_class = 30`. Fire when the cell at `t` differs from the initialisation cell,
  **sustained m = 3 consecutive windows**, **and** the change survives a bootstrap: resample
  the window **1,000×**, require the cell label stable in **≥ 90 %** of resamples. **Without
  the bootstrap, ordinary sampling noise around a boundary will fire C6 constantly.**
- **C7**: **all** of — no other cause fired; the forecast-acceptability gate passes (RMSSE
  below its own `W_svc`-window baseline, `|z_b| ≤ 1.96`, coverage within `τ_cov`); the
  service gap `ĉ < α_target − δ_svc` over `W_svc = 30`; **≥ 20 usable periods AND ≥ 2 review
  cycles with unmet demand** within `W_svc`.

**Confidence score.** `conf = min(1, max_over_fired( |statistic|/threshold ) − 0.5·(number of
causes that fired))`. The exact functional form is an implementer choice. **The required
properties**: monotone in evidence strength; decreasing when several causes fire together;
calibrated so `conf < τ_conf` is rare on the C0 control panel.

**Abstain (C0)** when `conf < τ_conf` (default 0.35), or when evidence is insufficient.

> **⛔ `τ_conf` tuning discipline.** The ≤ 10 % FP figure is a **design target that `τ_conf`
> is tuned to** — report it as a design characteristic, not an independent result. Tune
> `τ_conf` on **train**, report its value on **validation**, and treat the test panel as
> **confirmatory only**. **State this ordering explicitly in the paper.**

**Rules before classifier.** `attribution/rules.py` is the paper's method and must be
**readable by a reviewer**. The classifier is an optional layer on top, **evaluated against
the rules**, not instead of them.

R0–R7 exactly as §2.3. **Every remedy must pass a unit test proving it measurably changes

### 7.8 Inventory policy, service/cost reward, and the bandit (M4)

- **Policy:** order-up-to `S_{i,t} = Q̂_{α_t}(i,t)`, `α_t` from the R7 controller when R7 is
  active, else `α*`.
- **Reward:** §2.8, with the σ-floor. **Never accuracy.**
- **Bandit:** Thompson sampling, linear contextual, **one global policy** across all SKUs.
  ~300,000 decision points is ample.
- **Forgetting (required)** — default (a): on a C5 CUSUM alarm for SKU *i*, inflate that SKU's
  posterior variance for the affected arms, or discard the last `W_detect` observations from
  its sufficient statistics. Alternative (b) per-period discount `γ ≈ 0.99` is the E6
  comparison.
  **Why it is required:** C5 and C6 *are* non-stationarity injections. A standard Thompson
  sampler's posterior precision grows with each observation, so its variance collapses and it
  **stops exploring exactly when the world has changed** — CAFR would systematically
  under-perform on the causes it exists to handle.
  > **⛔ HARD REQUIREMENT — the forgetting mechanism must be IDENTICAL on (f), (f′) and A4.**
  > If A4 adapts at a different speed, the H1′ comparison measures adaptation speed rather
  > than attribution. **Unit-testable.**
- **Churn control:** dwell 3, confidence gate, R0 — §2.9.
- **Exploration:** staggered, §2.10.

### 7.9 Oracle replay (E7)

Because the simulator is deterministic given state, at any decision point **fork** it and run
every arm from the same state. The best of those is the oracle action;
`regret = cost(chosen) − cost(oracle)`.

**Four conditions (OD-9, frozen):**
1. **Horizon ≥ `R = L + 1` periods, discounted.** State both in the paper. A one-period
   horizon flatters R0 and penalises R7 (a safety-factor change pays off over many periods),
   biasing the very comparison the metric was added to strengthen.
2. **Identical future demand draws across arms**, asserted explicitly — **not** assumed from
   the seeding rule. A naive running-RNG implementation breaks this silently.
3. **Stratify the 5,000 points by cause and by panel**, and log them so the sample is
   auditable.
4. If compute blocks, **reduce to 1,000 points before dropping the metric.**

**Rename it in the paper**: a **sampled finite-horizon regret estimate**, not regret in the
online-learning sense.

| **(f) vs A4** | **Only the context block**: (f) gets `x = [x^inv, x^det]`, A4 gets `x^inv` only | Same action space R0–R7, same reward, same standardisation, **same action schedule**, same dwell, same confidence gate, **same forgetting mechanism** |
| **(f) vs (g)** | (g) has no learning — always apply the attributed cause's remedy | Same action space, same attribution, same churn machinery, matched churn |
| **(f) vs A5** | **Only the reward content**: (f) uses cost/service, A5 uses `−\|e_t\|/σ_{e,i}` | Same action space, **same standardisation with the same σ-floor** |
| **(f) vs (h)** | (h) has R0 removed | Same everything else |
| **(f) vs (f′)** | (f′) is constrained to the attributed cause's remedy + R0 | Same everything else |

> **⛔ The four hard constraints on A4. Violating any of them INVALIDATES the H1′ comparison:**
> 1. Same action space `{R0, …, R7}`.
> 2. Same reward (the OD-3 standardised cost reward).
> 3. **Same action schedule.** A4 must NOT be reduced to "act less often" or "act only on a
>    fixed timer". It receives the same dwell, hysteresis, confidence gate and forgetting
>    mechanism. **If A4's action frequency differs from (f)'s, the comparison measures action
>    frequency, not attribution.**
> 4. **Report realised churn for both arms in T3**, so a reader can verify constraint 3 held.
>    This is a reported diagnostic, not a constraint to impose.

### 8.3 The negative control — run this BEFORE trusting any positive result

**Arm (c) vs arm (c), with different random seeds, must show a NULL difference (95 % CI
includes zero).** If it does not, **the harness has a bug.** §30 gate 3.4 and §22.4 item 6.

### 8.4 Notes that keep baselines fair

- **(c) is the paper.** Implement it as faithfully as possible to how practitioners actually
  do it: rolling-window accuracy evaluation, switch-on-improvement with a margin, no churn
  constraint. **Do not handicap it. If (c) is weak, the result is worthless.**
- **(d)** uses EMC's dual thresholds (δ ∈ [0.1, 0.3], η ∈ [0.25, 0.5]) with the graded regime
  categories Stable → Watch → Transitional → Unstable, and memory-depth lowpass gating. Tune
  δ, η on validation.
- **(e)** must be a *fair* ML baseline: pooled training, static SKU features, lagged demand.
  **No SKU-specific training — that would leak.**

### 10.6 The success criteria for the hypotheses (Rev 2 §29 — pre-register before the test run)

> **Sign convention, used without exception: the reported difference between arms X and Y is
> `cost(X) − cost(Y)` — positive means X is worse.** So `(c) − (f) > 0` means (f) is better,
> and `(f) − A4 > 0` means (f) is better.

| ID | Role | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| **H1** | **Precondition** (necessary, not sufficient) | At matched model churn, (f) achieves a better inventory cost–service trade-off than (c). **Reported as confirmation of the cited benchmark (ρ = −0.555), not as the scientific claim.** |
| **H1′** | **★ CO-PRIMARY — the scientific claim** | At matched churn, (f) — the bandit **with attribution in its context** — beats **A4** — the same bandit, same action space R0–R7, same reward, **same action schedule**, attribution features removed |
| **H1″** | Secondary (RQ2) | At matched churn, (f) ≥ (g) — the learned mapping beats the fixed hand-written table |

**H1 — SUPPORTED if all hold on the test split:**

1. The paired mean total-cost difference **(c) − (f) is positive** with a **95 % bootstrap CI
   excluding zero** (clustered by SKU, and repeated clustered by generator parameter cell);
2. The **effect size is ≥ 2 %** of arm (c)'s mean cost — below 2 % is reported as **"no
   practical difference"** (the ROPE), regardless of `p`;
3. At **≥ 2 of the 3** swept target α ∈ {0.80, 0.90, 0.95}, (f) is better than (c); **and at
   no α is (c) significantly better after Holm correction**;
4. The result survives the **seed sweep** (≥ 4 of 5 seeds in the same direction) and the
   **±20 % threshold perturbation**.

**H1′ — SUPPORTED if all hold on the test split:**

1. The paired mean total-cost difference **(f) − A4 is positive** with a **95 % bootstrap CI
   excluding zero** (clustered by SKU, and repeated clustered by generator parameter cell);
2. The effect size is **≥ 2 %** of A4's mean cost;
3. **(f) is better than A4 at ≥ 2 of the 3** swept target α, and at **no** α is A4
   significantly better after Holm correction;
4. The result survives the **seed sweep** (≥ 4 of 5) and the **±20 % threshold perturbation**;
5. **Per-cause recall is reported twice** — pooled, and restricted to SKUs where
   `Δ_actual ≥ Δ_min`. **The budget-restricted numbers are the methodologically meaningful
   ones**; the pooled numbers are reported for completeness;
6. **(f) − A4 is computed with the same forgetting mechanism on both arms.** A difference in
   adaptation speed is not a difference in attribution.

**FAILED** if the difference is not positive, or the CI includes zero, or the frontier
reverses. **Report the failure.** A clearly-stated negative result on a well-posed question is
a legitimate paper.

**Secondary:** **H2** attribution macro-F1 ≥ 0.70 over C1–C7 **and** min per-class F1 ≥ 0.40
**and** FP rate ≤ 10 % on C0. **H3** (f) beats (g) with a positive CI, or report the
policy–table agreement rate and write it up as "the attribution alone carries the gain".
**H4** — descriptive only: report the non-forecast share (R2/R7/R0) with a bootstrap CI and
state whether the CI excludes zero. **The old "≥ 25 % target" is withdrawn** — it turned an
outcome of the data into a design target. **H5** ablation A5 is **worse** than (f) with a CI
excluding zero.

**Explicitly NOT required for success:** CAFR need not win on **forecast accuracy** (if it
does not, that is *expected* — and it must **not** be used as evidence for H1 or H1′); CAFR
need not win on every panel; attribution need not be perfect.

**What would falsify the whole approach:** if attribution macro-F1 over C1–C7 is **below
0.50**, the premise fails — the mechanisms are not separable, and **RQ1's answer is no**.
That is a publishable negative finding about the limits of forecast-failure diagnosis, but it
is **a different paper** from the one planned. Say so plainly rather than reporting a
degraded version of the original claim.

> **★ THE PRE-COMMITMENT (§29.4 — the single most important sentence in this section).**
> **If H1′ fails while H1 holds, the paper's contribution is the reward signal and the action
> space, not the attribution — and the paper says so in exactly those words.**

**Six design rules that are not optional:**

1. **One interface per module.** `Forecaster.fit(y, fit_on)/predict`, `Remedy.apply`,
   `Bandit.select/update`. **Every arm is a composition, not a copy-paste.**
2. **Rules before classifier.** `attribution/rules.py` is the paper's method and must be
   readable by a reviewer.
3. **No global state.** The simulator owns the state; the policy receives a read-only view.
4. **Everything config-driven.** **No magic numbers in code.** Every threshold appears in a
   YAML file and in T7.
5. **Notebooks compute nothing reported.**
6. **★ One feature list, one place.** Every arm builds its context from
   `configs/features.yaml`. **No arm constructs its own feature frame** — that is what makes
   the whitelist test meaningful.

**Libraries — free and open-source only, no GPU anywhere** (Rev 2 §26): `numpy`, `pandas`,
`scipy`, `statsmodels`, `scikit-learn`, `lightgbm`, `statsforecast`, `matplotlib`, `seaborn`,
`pyyaml`, `joblib`, `tqdm`, `pyarrow`, `pytest`. Optional: `pandera`, `river`, `sktime`.
**Explicitly excluded: anything requiring a paid licence, a GPU, or a cloud account.** All
compute is CPU and available on a laptop.
