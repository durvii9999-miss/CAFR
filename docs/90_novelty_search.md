## A. EXISTING WORK

### A1. A forecast-error taxonomy ALREADY EXISTS — in econometrics
This is the single most important finding, because it pre-empts our original headline claim.

- Clements & Hendry, *A General Forecast-Error Taxonomy* (2000)
- Hendry & Mizon, *An Open-Model Forecast-Error Taxonomy* (2011)
- Hendry & Mizon, *Open-Model Forecast-Error Taxonomies*, Springer 2012 — DOI `10.1007/978-1-4614-1653-1_9`

**Structure:** 3 model components (unobserved terms / observed stochastic variables / deterministic terms) × 3 problems (estimation uncertainty / mis-specification / change in DGP parameter values) = **9 sources of forecast error**.
**Headline result:** structural change is "the prime culprit" for forecast failure.
**Established cause-specific remedy:** **intercept corrections** — add the period-*T* residual at each forecast step. Castle, Doornik & Hendry use impulse indicators, then step indicators.

**What it is NOT:** macroeconometric series (continuous, dense, non-negative GDP/inflation). No intermittency, no inventory, no service level, no online automation, no empirical attribution from data.

> **Consequence:** "we introduce a forecast-failure taxonomy" is **taken**. The idea of pairing an error *source* with a *matched* remedy is also taken (intercept corrections). Our novelty cannot live there.

### A2. Intermittent demand already has a taxonomy — but it classifies the SERIES, not the failure
- **Syntetos–Boylan:** ADI × CV² → smooth / erratic / intermittent / lumpy (ADI cut-off 1.32).
- **Rožanec:** type **R** (regular occurrence — size model suffices) vs type **C+R** (irregular occurrence); four architectures — I: single size model, II: aggregate, III: separate occurrence + size models, IV: separate interval + size models.

These drive method **initialisation**. They answer "what does this series look like?", not "why did this forecast fail?"

### A3. Intermittent-demand error measurement already goes past accuracy
- MAD / MAE / MSE / RMSE / **MASE**, **GMAE** (Syntetos & Boylan 2005)
- **MASEII** (accounts for occurrence misclassification), **SPEC** (inventory impact), **AUC-ROC** on the occurrence process
- **PIS** (Periods In Stock) and **NOS** (Number of Shortages) — PCA shows these are *orthogonal* to MAD/MSE: accuracy measures simply do not carry the service information
- **Bias-Direction Diagnostic (BDD)**

### A4. "Accuracy ≠ service" is already established — do not claim it
- **arXiv 2609.13840**, *Accuracy Is Not Service: A Decision-Aware Benchmark for Intermittent-Demand Forecasting* — Spearman ρ = **−0.555** between accuracy rank and contractual service target across 38 methods (strengthens to −0.88 when near-duplicate variants are collapsed). Also ships a zero-training-cost instance-normalisation fix worth **+14.5 pts** fill (industrial panel) and **+17.1 pts** (RAF) at the 90% target.
- **arXiv 2601.21844** (Alabdallah et al., 2026), *Bridging Forecast Accuracy and Inventory KPIs* — closed-loop simulation framework; accuracy gains do not transfer to cost/service (reports Simpson's paradox). Open source.
- **SSRN 5554492** (Mikkonen & Saarinen, 2025), *Cost-Aligned Deep Learning for Intermittent Spare-Parts Demand and Inventory* — asymmetric cost-weighted loss, CSL sweep 75–95%.

> **Consequence:** "closed-loop / adaptive / decision-aware / cost-aligned" is **taken**. The user's warning is confirmed by the literature itself.

### A5. Adaptive / regime-specific methods for spare parts exist
- **AI-BA** (ACM, DOI `10.1145/3789982.3790048`): regime-specific adaptive ensemble; ADI/CV² classification → quantile forecasts aligned to service/reorder decisions.
- **Lyubchyk & Grinberg (2024)**, SSRN `10.2139/ssrn.4608877`: adaptive bias-corrected Croston variant. *(Closest single hit — but no diagnosis step.)*
- **Killi (2026)**, IJAIML, DOI `10.51483/ijaiml.6.5s.2026.548-555`: automated drift detection + inventory in micro-fulfilment. **Has 0 references** — low-quality-venue flag; cite cautiously, do not lean on it.

### A6. Drift detection already has churn-control machinery
- **EMC:** dual thresholds — drift δ ∈ [0.1, 0.3] *and* mode-similarity η ∈ [0.25, 0.5]; fast lead (λf ≈ 0.9) vs slow lead (λs ≈ 0.99) learning; periodic memory checks.
- **Graded regime categories:** Stable → Watch → Transitional → Unstable (four levels, not a binary switch).
- **Memory-depth lowpass gating** — explicitly to stop rapid switching when SNR is low.
- CUSUM accumulation, hysteresis, coherence-across-channels confirmation.

> **Consequence:** "avoid unnecessary model switching" is **taken as machinery**. It can only survive in our work as a *control variable*.

### A7. "Diagnose, then correct" exists — in adjacent fields, not this one
- **Exception-based forecasting** (JMIS 2014 `10.2753/mis0742-1222310209`; IJPDLM 2015 `10.1108/ijpdlm-02-2014-0017`): flag → **human** review. No automated cause attribution.
- **MPC prediction-error diagnosis** (Asian J. Control 2013 `10.1002/asjc.782`; ACC 2012 `10.1109/acc.2012.6314957`): same loop shape (error → diagnosis → remedy), different field (control engineering).
- **Maintenance / MRO side:** technician-confirmation loops retrain a *parts classifier* (e.g. open-source `TTOD-SOL/maintenance-intelligence-system`); Verdantis MRO360 reads free-text root-cause notes into a demand forecast; IJPE 2019 `10.1016/j.ijpe.2019.08.001` validates service-BOM alerts via an expert and feeds the corrections back (4–5% fewer non-availabilities/yr). All of these correct **data or fault records with human validation** — none attributes a *forecast* failure automatically and picks a remedy.
- **Alert patents** (e.g. US20130332233A1): error exceeds a threshold → alert. No cause classification.
- **Manary, Willems & Shihata**, *Interfaces* 2009 (Intel) — the strongest **academic** precedent we hold. Diagnoses forecast bias / nonnormal error / heterogeneous error, then applies a **targeted correction**. This is genuinely "diagnose-then-fix" in demand planning. But: not intermittent demand, no cause taxonomy, no automated loop, no inventory feedback signal, and human-authored. **Must be cited and distinguished.**
- **RELEX Solutions**, "AI-assisted diagnostics" (product launch, Nov 2025) — a commercial tool that identifies root causes and recommends corrective actions. Industry ships this; it is not literature and publishes no method. Cite as evidence the *market* wants this, which strengthens rather than weakens our case.

### A8. Meta-learning "when and how to adapt" exists
- **CoSFan** (ICLR 2025): meta-learns *what, how & when* to adapt latent neural dynamics. Not demand, not causes, not remedies.
- Online-within-online meta-learning; loss-threshold adaptation triggers.

### A9. ⚠️ THE CLOSEST NEIGHBOUR YET — "Predict-then-Correct" (PtC) *(found round 3)*
**Lei, Ma & Jackson, arXiv `2607.16354`, July 2026** — *A Predict-then-Correct Loop Based on Few-Shot Continuous Contextual Bandit for Demand Forecasting.*

A bandit-driven correction loop bolted onto a demand forecast, evaluated on M5 plus an industrial beverage panel spanning "erratic & intermittent" demand, reporting **lower inventory costs** than base-stock, PPO and SAC. This is the paper a reviewer will raise first. Verified against the **full text**, not the abstract:

- **No attribution.** No module explains why the baseline failed; the context carries only a raw error summary ("Historical ML error").
- **Action space is one number.** "the action is defined as the continuous adjustment factor applied to the ML-predicted demand", clipped to [−1, 2] — a multiplicative correction, not a remedy set.
- **Reward is accuracy, not service.** "the reward is defined as the relative degree of improvement after correction", `r_t = τ(e_ML,t − e_CB,t)`; "maximizing the reward is equivalent to minimizing the corrected prediction error." Inventory cost appears only as a downstream *comparison*, never as the learning signal.
- **Lumpy SKUs deliberately excluded**: "lumpy demand has long zero-demand periods and irregular spikes, making statistical and AI/ML models largely ineffective."
- No churn control; no occurrence/size split.

> **Consequence:** PtC is best understood as a **degenerate single-action case of CAFR's action space** — one remedy, chosen on an accuracy reward, without ever asking *why*. It does not take the contribution; it **must be cited first** and distinguished on all four criteria.

### A10. ⚠️ A patent that already couples cause → forecast *and* policy action *(found round 3)*
**US20100125489** — *System and Method for Root Cause Analysis and Early Warning of Inventory Problems.*

Described actions include "adjustments to demand forecasts" **and** "adjustments to published inventory policy parameters (such as safety stock targets)"; the description calls the result "self-tuning with each decision-action loop turning into a cycle of learning", a "Plan-Do-Check-Act process that facilitates an auto-learning and self-tuning system."

**Real limits:** root-cause language sits in **dependent claim 7/14 and the description**, not the independent claims, which recite only "optimize the inventory policy parameters"; it **never mentions spare parts or intermittent demand**; the planner "confirms root cause via collaboration and consensus"; causes are prioritised "according to business rules"; and there is no mechanism taxonomy. *(Content summary, not legal advice.)*

> **Consequence:** this is prior art for "diagnose → adjust forecasts *and* policy parameters → loop", and it is closer to CAFR's **non-forecast action space** than anything in the literature. Cite and distinguish. It is **not** prior art for an automated, mechanism-level, intermittently-demand-specific, service-learned repair layer.

### A11. The remaining 2026 neighbours, clustered *(found round 3; detail in file `09` §2)*
All verified as **not threats**; each holds one fragment of CAFR.

- **LOWDII** — arXiv `2607.19835`: a "diagnostic that evaluates the distributional influence of each historical forecast error on the empirical uncertainty distribution", feeding safety-stock calibration; hits the target service level while cutting average stock 5.1–22.6 %. But it is a **statistical refinement, not a cause attribution**, in **FMCG, not intermittent demand**. It is the closest thing to CAFR's *cause-7* machinery and should be cited when justifying cause 7.
- **AHSIV / AHS** — arXiv `2602.13939`: adaptive automatic model selection "combines observed predictive performance with structural demand information" using RMSSE/MAE/sMAPE/BIAS. No attribution, no non-forecast actions, **no churn control**, not intermittent. This *is* the state of the art in adaptive selection — cite it as the strong form of experiment arm (c)/(d).
- **LCMA** — KBS 2026, `10.1016/j.knosys.2026.116334`: LLM multi-agent framework for large-scale spare parts; a "Model Selection Agent — uses a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) mechanism to dynamically match forecasting models to demand patterns." It classifies **demand patterns**, not **failures**, and its only action is model↔pattern matching. **It is the paradigm CAFR argues against, and its whole design is dynamic switching — so churn control is not merely absent from this literature, it is contravened by it.** Use it as the foil.
- **arXiv `2510.01006`**: after-sales loop "closing the loop between forecasting, monitoring, and inventory decisions" over ~6,000 parts; "ranked root causes"; "bias decomposition (over- vs under-forecast)". Diagnostics are bias/lifecycle/regime only, **and no action set is ever enumerated** — it asks "which levers should we pull?" without listing them. Objective is WMAPE, not service/cost.
- **NNARMA** — EJOR 2026, `10.1016/j.ejor.2026.06.009`: non-negative ARMA for intermittent demand with prediction intervals "suitable for stock control applications". Confirms REJ-9 — but the **forecast→inventory** link is what is studied; the **failure→cause→remedy** link is not.
- **Sensors 2023**, `10.3390/s23167182`: adaptive interval prediction with a dynamic update mechanism. Interval *widening* exists off the shelf as a method — it does **not** exist as a *diagnosed* remedy. This is a **strength**: CAFR need not invent its remedies, only the selection of them.
- **Zenodo `10.5281/zenodo.18364003`**: the **only** prior statement of a no-action option found anywhere — "some operational processes are not well-suited for automated forecasting and are better managed through expert oversight." A closing remark in a non-peer-reviewed notebook, not a learned action. Cite as the one precedent for the *idea* that abstaining is sometimes right.
- **SHOS** — Sci Rep 2026, `10.1038/s41598-026-57129-6`: occurrence/size structure as **feature engineering** (MAE −46.3 %), not as diagnosis. Third independent confirmation that the split is used for *modelling*, never for *attribution*.
- **Industry** — ToolsGroup (diagnose long-tail drivers before changing methods; limit overrides to stop human overreaction), PTC/Servigistics ("systemic or macro-level issues requiring tuning of the planning model itself"), Blue Yonder ("Install Base & Causal Forecasting"). Reinforces A7: **the market keeps reaching for diagnose-then-act and ships it human-in-the-loop, and no vendor publishes a method.**
- **ISNCC 2026**, `10.1109/ISNCC70543.2026.11693859` — *Stock-Out Distortion and Under-Forecasting: An Inventory-Aware Diagnosis of Demand-Signal Bias in Multi-SKU Sales Forecasting* (Amarta). **Found by the round-3 IEEE search** and the clearest recent sign that the field is *starting* to ask why forecasts fail. It diagnoses **one** cause — stock-out censoring, where "the signal used to train forecasting models is distorted" — and links it to systematic under-forecasting across SARIMA, XGBoost (Tweedie) and CNN-LSTM on 33 lubricant SKUs. It then **stops at the diagnosis**: no remedy selection, no action space, no learning loop. Two points to use in the introduction: (i) its own finding is that the effect **weakens on intermittent SKUs** "whose zeros are largely structural" — i.e. it applies *where CAFR does not*, and CAFR's subject is exactly where its mechanism fails; (ii) it is the cleanest evidence that authors are now diagnosing forecast failure and that the field currently goes no further.

### THE SEAM THAT IS STILL OPEN (stated precisely)

No work was found that does **all four** of these together:

1. classifies **why an intermittent-demand forecast failed**, by mechanism;
2. maps each mechanism to a **matched corrective action — which may lie outside the forecast** (widen the interval, adjust the inventory policy, or do nothing) rather than always changing the model;
3. **learns that cause→remedy mapping from downstream inventory service/cost feedback**, not from forecast accuracy;
4. does so while **explicitly controlling model churn**.

Every existing piece holds one or two. **None held all four.** That intersection is our gap.

**Round 3 re-tested this seam from 18 further OpenAlex angles and ~12 web searches. It survived.** The nearest work per criterion, after round 3:

### GAP-2 (BACKUP — smaller, independently publishable) — Separating forecast failure from policy failure

**Question:** When the achieved service level misses target, can we tell whether the *forecast* is at fault or the *inventory policy* is, and does that separation improve the cost–service trade-off?

- **Existing:** "Accuracy Is Not Service" (A4) proves the two are decoupled, and the *caveat* it reports — "under a complete-order service contract the same bias can improve service; removing it for accuracy can reduce service" — hints at the mechanism but does not build the discriminator.
- **Missing:** an operational test that discriminates the two causes and routes the response accordingly.
- **Dataset / cost / difficulty:** same as GAP-1. Slightly easier.
- **Rosiest risk:** reviewers may call it a diagnostic study rather than a method. Its strength is that it is small enough to finish and testable on RAF alone.

### GAP-3 (SEPARABLE COMPONENT) — Occurrence-failure vs size-failure attribution

**Question:** Can a forecast failure be attributed specifically to the **occurrence sub-process** or the **size sub-process**, and does that attribution change which remedy helps?

- **Existing:** the split exists for **modelling** (Croston separation; Rožanec types III/IV; hurdle models) and for **measurement** (MASEII vs MASE; AUC-ROC on occurrence). The *diagnosis-then-repair* use of the split was not found.
- **Missing:** using the occurrence/size split as a **diagnostic** that selects the remedy (occurrence failure → probability-based method; size failure → size-model re-estimation).
- **Note:** this is the sharpest and most defensible sub-piece, and it is *small enough to complete*. Recommended as an explicit deliverable inside GAP-1, not as a standalone project.

## D. FINAL RECOMMENDED RESEARCH QUESTION

> For intermittent industrial spare-parts demand, does attributing each forecast failure to a specific mechanism — **occurrence failure, size failure, level shift, intermittency change, uncertainty mis-calibration, or inventory-policy mis-set** — and applying the *matched* corrective action, produce a better inventory cost–service trade-off than selecting a forecasting model by forecast accuracy, **at the same level of model churn**?

**Sub-questions**
- **RQ1 (attribution):** can these mechanisms be separated reliably from observable residual + inventory signals alone, without knowing the true data-generating process?
- **RQ2 (learning):** does a cause→remedy mapping *learned* from service/cost feedback beat a fixed, hand-written mapping?
- **RQ3 (decomposition):** how much of the gain comes from **non-forecast** remedies (policy adjustment, interval widening, no-action) versus forecast-model changes?

## E. EXACT NOVEL CONTRIBUTION (one sentence)

> **We introduce a cause-attributed forecast-repair layer for intermittent spare-parts demand that classifies each forecast failure by mechanism — separating occurrence failure, size failure, level shift, intermittency change, uncertainty mis-calibration, and inventory-policy mis-set — and selects the matched corrective action, where the cause→remedy mapping is learned from downstream inventory service-level feedback rather than from forecast accuracy.**

**Falsifiable claim:** at equal model churn, cause-attributed repair beats accuracy-based model selection on the inventory cost–service frontier.

**Honest scoping sentence (put this in the report):**
> No directly matching work was found in the searched databases (OpenAlex, IEEE Xplore, Crossref, web search); Scopus and Web of Science were not searched. The individual components exist separately — in econometric forecast-error taxonomy (Clements & Hendry), intermittent-demand theory (Syntetos–Boylan, Croston), decision-aware benchmarking (arXiv 2609.13840, ICDM-26), bandit-driven forecast correction (PtC, arXiv 2607.16354), and root-cause-to-action inventory patents (US20100125489) — and each of those holds at most two of CAFR's four elements. This work is, to the best of our search, the first to couple them into an operational repair loop for intermittent spare-parts inventory.

## F. PROPOSED SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

**M0 — Base forecaster pool.** Croston, SBA, TSB, mSBA, mTSB, SES on nonzero sizes, a global LightGBM. Cheap, all reproducible.

**M1 — Outcome monitor.** Per SKU, per review period: rolling residual, bias sign and magnitude, empirical prediction-interval coverage, ADI and CV², CUSUM on the residual mean, and the realised inventory outcome (CSL, fill rate, backorders, PIS/NOS).

**M2 — Attribution module.** Maps the M1 signals to one of six causes + a confidence score. Rules first (auditable), a small classifier over M1 features as a refinement.

**M3 — Remedy library.** One matched action per cause, plus a deliberate *no-action*.

**M4 — Remedy-selection policy.** Contextual bandit (Thompson sampling) or a learned decision table. Reward = change in inventory cost/service over the review period, **not accuracy**. *(The bandit itself is not novel — M21 confirmed this: "Contextual Bandits for Evaluating and Improving Inventory Control Policies" (arXiv 2310.16096) and PtC (arXiv 2607.16354) both already put bandits on inventory/demand. Say so openly. The novelty is the action space + the reward signal, not the bandit.)*

**M5 — Evaluation harness.** Simulated periodic-review inventory; rolling-origin backtest; logs churn.

### The attribution table — the technically precise core

| Cause | Observable signature (M1) | Matched remedy | Resembles (existing) | What is new here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Persistent directional bias** | Bias sign stable over k windows, in *both* occurrence and size | Intercept correction / bias-adjusted estimator | SBA; intercept corrections (Clements–Hendry) | Applied **selectively**, only when attributed — not as a global rule |
| **2. Uncertainty mis-calibration** | Empirical interval coverage < nominal | Conformal recalibration / widen the interval | Conformal prediction | Triggered by **diagnosis**, not on a schedule |
| **3. Occurrence failure** | AUC-ROC on occurrence low; false-zero rate high; size model fine | Switch to a probability-based architecture (TSB / hurdle) | TSB; Rožanec type III | Attribution **between the two sub-processes** |
| **4. Size failure** | Given occurrence correct, nonzero-size error high | Re-estimate the size model (damped trend on nonzero sizes) | Croston variant space | Same as above |
| **5. Level / regime shift** | CUSUM on residual mean sustained; step indicator significant | Shorten the estimation window + step indicator; drop stale data | Intercept corrections; EMC drift flag | Coupled to **this specific remedy**, not to a generic "adapt" |
| **6. Intermittency change** | ADI and/or CV² persistently crosses the Syntetos–Boylan boundary | Re-classify and re-initialise the method | Syntetos–Boylan classification | Classification used as a **diagnosis trigger**, not as initialisation |
| **7. Policy mis-set** | Forecast acceptable but the CSL gap persists at current (S, S) | Adjust the safety factor / reorder point — **leave the forecast alone** | Safety-stock theory | Explicitly a **non-forecast** remedy in the action space |
| **8. No actionable signal** | Attribution confidence below threshold | **Do nothing** (persistence) | Hysteresis / memory gating | Controls churn by design; an **ablation target** |

Cause 7 is the one that operationalises the "Accuracy Is Not Service" reversal: sometimes the forecast is fine and the *policy* is wrong — and no forecasting paper can fix that by changing the forecast.

## H. EXPERIMENT / BASELINES

**Inventory simulation:** periodic review, order-up-to (S, S). Target CSL ∈ {80%, 90%, 95%}. Holding cost h and stockout/backorder cost b swept. 84 periods, rolling origin, per-SKU.

**Arms**

| Arm | What it is | Role |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Fixed best classical method (Croston / SBA / TSB), per SKU | Classical baseline |
| (b) | Syntetos–Boylan rule-based selection | Current practice |

**Metrics**

- Control: **number of switches** — hold equal for the (c) vs (f) headline comparison
- Attribution: per-cause precision/recall on the synthetic panel

**Statistics:** paired per-SKU comparisons; bootstrap CIs on the cost–service difference.

**The headline figure:** cost–service frontier, with (c) and (f) at matched churn. If (f) does not dominate (c), the claim fails — state this in advance.
